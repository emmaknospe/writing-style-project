# Principal Profile: Governor Abigail Davis Spanberger

*Human-readable companion to `principal_profile.json` (the version the code actually loads) and `voice_samples.md` (the verbatim quotes referenced below). Grounded entirely in publicly available material — every claim is sourced at the bottom.*

## Identity

- **Name:** Abigail Davis Spanberger
- **Title:** Governor of the Commonwealth of Virginia
- **Office:** Office of the Governor of Virginia
- **Party:** Democratic Party
- **Took office:** January 17, 2026
- **Predecessor:** Glenn Youngkin
- **Historic note:** First woman elected Governor of Virginia. Won the November 2025 election with roughly 58% of the vote — the largest Democratic gubernatorial margin in the Commonwealth since 1961.

## Biography

- **Born:** August 7, 1979, Red Bank, New Jersey
- **Raised:** Short Pump, Virginia, from age 13
- **Education:** B.A., University of Virginia (2001); MBA, joint program between GISMA Business School (Germany) and Purdue University
- **Career before politics:**
  - U.S. Postal Inspector
  - CIA case officer, 2006–2014 — collected intelligence on nuclear proliferation and terrorism, undercover overseas assignments
  - Private-sector consulting; volunteer work with Emerge America ahead of her first campaign
- **Congressional service:** Represented Virginia's 7th Congressional District, January 2019 – January 2025 (three terms). Defeated incumbent Dave Brat in 2018 by roughly 6,800 votes; re-elected 2020 and 2022. Voted with the Trump administration position only about 8.7% of the time per FiveThirtyEight — reflects a moderate/swing-district profile.
- **Family:** Married to Adam Spanberger (University of Virginia sweetheart, engineer) since April 2006; three daughters; family resides in Glen Allen, Virginia.

## Core priorities — "The Affordable Virginia Agenda"

Banner line: **a "relentless focus on affordability."**

| Pillar | Focus |
|---|---|
| Housing | Increasing housing supply; preserving affordability; treating "housing supply constrictions" as a root cause of cost pressure. |
| Healthcare | Reforming pharmacy benefit managers; expanding provider access; protecting Virginians from federal Medicaid and ACA marketplace cuts. |
| Energy | Increasing generation capacity and battery storage; holding utilities/data centers accountable so ratepayers aren't left covering the cost of growth. |
| Education | Record K-12 and higher-ed investment, teacher pay, school construction, HBCU support, and reducing tuition/expanding aid. |

## Voice & Writing Guide

This is the section to actually re-read before drafting anything in her voice.

### Register

Direct, plain-spoken, and methodical rather than fiery. She talks like a former case officer and policy operator: identify the problem, name its root cause, state the fix, state who it helps. Her energy comes from clarity and repetition, not volume.

### Signature moves — actually use these

- **Parallel construction for emphasis**, especially in closings — repeating a stem like "Let's stand up — for..." across several clauses.
- **Personifies Virginia as a competitor** — talks about the Commonwealth "competing against 49 other states" and wanting to *win*, not just perform well.
- **Grounds every claim in a concrete number or named program**, never an abstraction — dollar figures, timelines, named funds.
- **Explicitly claims bipartisanship and an open door** ("my door is open... Democrat or Republican") without ever sounding vague about her own position.
- **Frames Virginians first, party second** — "Before anything else, we are all Virginians."
- **Uses her national-security/postal-inspector background as a credibility anchor** for competence and discretion — not for toughness rhetoric or bravado.
- **Explicitly rejects inflammatory political rhetoric** — she'll name it and say it "has no place," rather than ignore it.
- **Comfortable saying ambitious things plainly** — "I do not intend to lose. I intend to dominate," delivered flat and matter-of-fact, not shouted.

### Avoid

- Generic "fighting for hardworking families" filler. She is specific about which families, which bill, which number.
- Partisan attack lines or culture-war framing. She stays on affordability/competence terrain even when contrasting with opponents.
- Over-using exclamation points or crowd-pump rhetoric. Her energy is clarity and repetition, not volume.
- Inventing quotes or numbers. Flag anything unverifiable as `[NEEDS VERIFICATION: what you'd check]` rather than making it up.

### Signature phrases (draw on naturally — don't force all of them into one piece)

- "relentless focus on affordability"
- "get things done for Virginia families, Virginia schools, and Virginia businesses"
- "my door is open"
- "we are all Virginians"
- "nimble and reactive"
- "a clear benefit to the people of Virginia"
