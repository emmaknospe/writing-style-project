Spanberger: “This Is Not a Rainy Day…This Is a Bad Bill That Came Out of Washington”

RICHMOND, Va. — Congresswoman Abigail Spanberger continues to stand up for healthcare access in rural Virginia amid devastating cuts imposed by the Republican tax law, legislation that her opponent Winsome Earle-Sears has praised.

In new reporting from CNN, Spanberger made clear that Virginia must find a long-term solution to address the “immense” challenges federal healthcare cuts present for Virginia families, rural communities, and providers.

Just this month, Augusta Health Group announced that three Virginia rural healthcare clinics in Weyers Cave, Churchville, and Buena Vista will close their doors due to the Trump tax law slashing support for Virginia’s rural healthcare providers. Additionally, the bill threatens healthcare access for more than 350,000 Virginians and will eliminate nearly $26 billion in funding for Virginia hospitals.

—

CNN: Rural health clinics are closing after Trump’s ‘One Big Beautiful Bill,’ raising the legislation’s political risks

Exactly two months after President Donald Trump signed his policy megabill in a July 4 celebration at the White House, a Virginia health care company blamed the law for the closure of three rural clinics serving communities along the Blue Ridge Mountains.

The closures, Augusta Medical Group said in its statement, were part of the company’s “ongoing response to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act and the resulting realities for healthcare delivery.”

[…]

Virginia Lt. Gov. Winsome Earle-Sears, the GOP nominee for governor, has also proposed tapping the state’s rainy day fund to help cover additional funding.

“We want to make sure that whatever happens with Medicaid, we have the money here to help. We have the money and the budget to help. You know, we have put money aside for rainy day,” Sears said at an event in Marion, according to a report from Cardinal News. “The bank account has never been that full. And so we are ready for any changes that happen.”

Spanberger says that rainy day fund — which outgoing GOP Gov. Glenn Youngkin said last month held $4.7 billion — won’t be enough.

“This is not a rainy day. This is a bad bill that came out of Washington,” Spanberger said on the first day of early voting in Virginia at an event in Fairfax on Friday. “They are throwing those costs on the state, and in the interim, people will fall off of their health care, so the problem is immense.”

BACKGROUND

Spanberger has a proven record of lowering healthcare and prescription drug costs for Virginia families and seniors. During her three terms in Congress, Spanberger helped lead the charge to give Medicare the power to negotiate for lower drug prices and save taxpayers billions of dollars in the process.

Spanberger also voted to cap insulin copays at $35 per month for seniors on Medicare and cap out-of-pocket drug costs for Virginia seniors. Additionally, President Trump signed into law Spanberger’s effort to make it harder for pharmaceutical industry middlemen — pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) — to rip off consumers.

Earlier this year, Spanberger announced her Affordable Virginia Plan, which is focused on lowering Virginians’ healthcare and prescription drug costs by cracking down on industry middlemen that drive up costs, holding large pharmaceutical companies accountable, improving price transparency, and expanding healthcare access in rural and underserved communities across the Commonwealth.

Last month, Spanberger and former Governor Ralph Northam — a pediatric neurologist at Children’s Hospital of The King’s Daughters in Norfolk — met with rural healthcare providers on the Eastern Shore to discuss the impacts of the Republican tax law on their ability to provide care.

According to the Virginia Department of Medical Assistance Services, the Republican tax law cuts approximately $26 billion in funding for Virginia hospitals. Across the Commonwealth, at least six rural hospitals — including one in Hampton Roads, one on the Northern Neck, and four in Southwest and Southside Virginia — are at risk of closing because of this law.
