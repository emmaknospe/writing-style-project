RICHMOND, VA — Governor Abigail Spanberger highlighted a new grant program — funded through an amendment the Governor proposed to the Commonwealth’s biennial budget — which invests $2 million each year to support localities and departments in providing cancer screenings for career firefighters. The budget amendment allows for $350 per eligible screening for career firefighters.

Cancer is the leading cause of death in the fire service. While serving in Congress, Governor Spanberger led bipartisan legislation to provide Veteran military firefighters with their earned healthcare benefits and disability compensation by creating the presumption that Veteran military firefighters who become disabled by serious diseases — including certain cancers — contracted their illness from their military service. Firefighters face an increased risk of cancer due to exposure to carcinogenic fumes, chemicals, and other contaminants.

A new state grant program aims to help protect Virginia firefighters from one of the deadliest risks they face on the job: cancer.

Virginia is investing $2 million as part of the two-year state budget to help career fire departments pay for specialized cancer screenings, giving firefighters access to early detection that many departments say has been financially out of reach.

“This program will save lives,” said William Boger, district vice president and communications director for the Virginia Professional Firefighters.

The National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health reports that firefighters are 9% more likely to develop cancer and 14% more likely to die from it than the general public. Repeated exposure to smoke, toxic chemicals and contaminants on firefighting gear contributes to the increased risk.
