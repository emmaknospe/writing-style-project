More Than 70 Percent of Virginians Believe There Is Not Enough Housing Available That They Can Actually Afford

Earlier This Year, Spanberger Rolled Out Her “Affordable Virginia Plan,” Which Includes Her Priorities for Lowering Housing Costs for Virginia Families

RICHMOND, Va — Congresswoman Abigail Spanberger’s clear vision to lower housing costs across the Commonwealth continues to resonate with Virginia families as new polling — highlighted in the Roanoke Times — shows high costs and housing affordability are a top issue for Virginia voters.

According to the poll, 37 percent of Virginians said that the high cost of housing is the number-one issue facing the Commonwealth — while 33 percent are most concerned about inflation and high costs across the board. More than 70 percent of Virginians believe there is not enough housing available in the Commonwealth that they can afford to rent or buy.

“No matter where Abigail travels across the Commonwealth, she hears from Virginians who are feeling squeezed by high housing costs and are ready for a Governor with real solutions to lower costs for both buyers and renters,” said Connor Joseph, Communications Director, Spanberger for Governor. “While her opponent — Winsome Earle-Sears — has no plan to lower housing costs, Abigail continues to talk every day about the concrete steps her administration will take to make sure every Virginia family can find a home that meets their needs and fits their budget.”

Last week, Spanberger discussed how her Affordable Virginia Plan will save Virginians money and help more families build a life here in the Commonwealth — including by making it easier for Virginia families, young people, and first-time homebuyers to find housing.

BACKGROUND

Earlier this year, Spanberger rolled out her plan to lower costs for Virginians — her Affordable Virginia Plan, which would lower healthcare, housing, and energy costs for Virginia families.

Spanberger has also announced her Growing Virginia Plan and Strengthening Virginia Schools Plan — showing that her administration will have a plan on day one to address challenges facing Virginia’s economy and schools.

As a member of Congress, Spanberger built a record of working with both Democrats and Republicans to deliver results for Virginians — and was named the most bipartisan Member of Congress from Virginia.
