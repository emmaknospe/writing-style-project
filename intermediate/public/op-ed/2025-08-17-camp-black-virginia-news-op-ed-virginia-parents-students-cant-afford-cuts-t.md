By Congresswoman Abigail Spanberger & Senator L. Louise Lucas

This week, students across the Commonwealth are heading back to school.

As mothers, we know firsthand the mix of excitement and nervous jitters that fills families’ homes this time of year. Between shopping for school supplies, meeting new teachers, and getting back in the swing of pick-ups and drop-offs, parents have a lot on their minds.

But for parents here in Hampton Roads and across every corner of Virginia, one thing is looming over their heads and seemingly out of their control: the chaos coming out of Washington.

Just last month, the Trump Administration was given the greenlight to slash the U.S. Department of Education in half, threatening everything from after-school programs to schools’ foundational ability to get high-quality teachers in the classroom.

As local schools and parents grapple with the consequences of these reckless threats, Virginia’s Governor and Lieutenant Governor are silent. No plans. No press conferences. No reassurances for Virginia parents.

Come January — with a strong majority in the General Assembly, Virginia Democrats are ready to get to work with a Governor who will sign our bills and budget to deliver for Virginia parents and students. That means investing in education at every level: our K-12 schools, colleges and universities, and the early education and child care programs that set children up for success.

Our main objective is making sure Virginia’s public schools are the best in America. Getting there starts with recognizing the serious challenges we face.

Virginia parents need more support from the very start. The data are clear: early childhood education is one of the smartest investments in our children’s futures. But right now, parents across Virginia are lacking access to high-quality, affordable early child care options. While the Trump Administration is threatening programs that have a proven record of success in Virginia, we will bring down child care costs for parents and bring more early childhood educators into classrooms.

Virginia is also contending with a chronic teacher shortage — and the White House is only making things worse. Earlier this year, the Trump Administration abruptly canceled a grant that would have helped local school districts partner with Norfolk State University to bring high-quality teachers and administrators into our classrooms — and keep them there.

Instead of making it harder for schools to find and hire qualified educators, we need to raise teacher salaries and create pathways to bring more Virginians into the profession. Investing in our educators is investing in our next generation — it’s how we set Virginia’s kids up for lifelong success.

With the right support, every student in Virginia can find a career path that works for them — whether that means going straight into the workforce, training in an apprenticeship program, attending a community college, joining the military, or attending a four-year university.

Unfortunately, instead of working with our General Assembly to expand opportunity for Virginia students, the Youngkin-Sears Administration has prioritized politicizing Virginia’s world-class colleges and universities. Our focus should be on upholding academic excellence at these institutions — including Virginia’s exceptional HBCUs, which have been chronically underfunded — and giving more students the chance to pursue a college degree.

The Republican nominee for Governor — Virginia’s current Lieutenant Governor, Winsome Earle-Sears, has a decades-long record of working to defund Virginia’s public schools. She has also backed the Trump Administration’s dangerous efforts to slash education programs. While Virginia ranked dead last in the country in math recovery from 2019 to 2024, she has offered no plan to improve student achievement. Investing in our students is investing in our Commonwealth’s future — and our communities and our economy can’t afford politicians who sit on the sidelines.

Parents often tell us that when watching the nightly news, they feel like protecting their families from chaos is out of their control. And right now, the power to stand up to the White House rests in a Governor, Lieutenant Governor, and Attorney General who aren’t up to the task.

But when early voting starts on September 19, the power to decide the direction of our Commonwealth rests with Virginians at the ballot box. This fall, Virginians have the power to choose leaders who are focused on investing in Virginia’s kids, supporting families, and planning for Virginia’s future, or leaders with no spine and no intention of putting Virginia’s students and parents first.

Senator L. Louise Lucas of Portsmouth is President Pro Tempore of the Virginia Senate. She also serves as Chair of the Senate Committee on Finance and Appropriations. She has served in the Senate since 1992.

Congresswoman Abigail Spanberger represented Virginia’s Seventh District in the U.S. House of Representatives from 2019 to 2025. She is now the Democratic nominee for Governor of Virginia.
