Washingtonian: “[Spanberger’s] Message is Something Like: As Governor, I’ll Keep You Safe. I’ll Grow Your Economy. I’ll Make Sure Your Kids Go to Decent Schools”

RICHMOND, Va. — In a new Washingtonian profile, Congresswoman Abigail Spanberger makes clear that she’s running for Governor to put Virginia families first.

The profile focuses on Spanberger’s career of public service, her bipartisan record of delivering real results for the Commonwealth as a Member of Congress, and her commitment to addressing the very real challenges facing Virginians — including by standing up to chaos coming out of Washington that is hurting families and businesses.

The Washingtonian laid out what led to Spanberger’s service as a federal agent and CIA case officer:

“Part of it was about values. Her parents, a nurse and a federal law-enforcement officer, instilled in her ‘a lot of reverence for the notion of protecting other people and upholding the law.’”

“At the University of Virginia, Spanberger studied French and foreign affairs, then moved to Germany for an MBA. Back then, in 2001, the CIA needed German speakers to help untangle the wreckage of the Cold War, so she planned to burnish her language skills before returning to the States. But one day at school, she was walking through a common room and heard the loud and urgent voice of an American. It was a news reporter on TV. Slowly, Spanberger registered what had happened: The first of the Twin Towers had fallen.”

“In 2002, Spanberger returned to the US, finished her MBA, and applied to the CIA. The Agency took her—but the post-9/11 hiring boom increased processing times, so she wasn’t set to start training for several years.”

“While she waited, Spanberger joined the US Postal Inspection Service, a federal law-enforcement agency that handles crimes related to the mail. There, she worked cases that involved narcotics, money laundering, child predation, and suspicious powders.”

“In 2006, Spanberger was finally called up to the CIA, and she operated stateside for a time before being sent overseas.”

—

The Washingtonian also underscored Spanberger’s focus on putting Virginians first — including by standing up to President Trump’s DOGE attacks and laying out her plans to grow Virginia’s economy:

“Spanberger is running as a moderate, a professional, a no-nonsense counterweight to the chaos in Washington and the dysfunction of the Virginia GOP. On the stump, [Spanberger] talks policy and plans. The message is something like: As governor, I’ll keep you safe. I’ll grow your economy. I’ll make sure your kids go to decent schools.”

“In late July, Spanberger was making a run-of-the-mill campaign stop inside a low-slung brick building belonging to the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers, in a suburban tangle of parking lots and strip malls by Manassas Battlefield Park.”

“‘I’m here today laying out our Growing Virginia Plan,’ she said, standing behind a lectern in a staid black skirt-suit and bright-green top. ‘My plan is built on’ […] ‘growing Virginia’s exceptional workforce’ and ‘growing business and economic investments in Virginia.’”

“‘Virginia is facing unprecedented attacks on our communities, on our economy, on our jobs right now,’ Spanberger told the crowd at the union hall. She brought up the Department of Government Efficiency’s slash-and-burn rampage through the federal bureaucracy, which has affected the state’s more than 320,000 federal workers and their families, plus the contractors and small-business owners whose livelihoods depend on them.”

“She called the Trump administration’s tariffs ‘a massive tax hike on Virginians’ and, in a media gaggle, mentioned June data from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, which showed that Virginia was “‘the only state in the country that saw statistically significant job loss.’”

—

The profile also highlighted the contrast in this year’s race for Governor — with Spanberger’s opponent supporting President Trump’s attacks on Virginia:

“On the trail, Spanberger frequently describes a state beset by the chaos of a Republican White House while its Republican governor is asleep at the wheel. It’s proved a tough attack for her opponent to parry.”

“Earle-Sears is in a tricky electoral position: defending the President in a state where about 55 percent of residents disapprove of him. It’s led to some gaffes. In March, for instance, audio leaked of her saying it’s ‘not unusual’ to lose a job—that it ‘happens to everybody all the time’—so she’s not sure why the media is treating DOGE as ‘this huge, huge thing.’ In August, she was asked on CNN if she supports federal-workforce cuts, and she wouldn’t answer. Earle-Sears can’t disavow Trump and risk alienating his base or provoking his wrath, but she’s struggling to embrace a domestic-policy agenda that UVA researchers have suggested could cost Virginia billions in GDP.”
