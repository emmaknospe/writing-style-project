By Henry Graff

RICHMOND, Va. (WWBT) – Abigail Spanberger campaigned at Forest Hill Park in Richmond over the weekend to rally voters around her ‘Getting things done for Virginia’ agenda.

“We need a governor who can try to steady things and create as much certainty as possible,” Abigail Spanberger, (D) Candidate for Virginia Governor, said.

The democratic nominee sat down with 12 On Your Side to talk about the issues facing the next governor of the commonwealth.

“The next governor is going to have to contend with the immediacy of challenges that we have seen because of the chaos coming out of Washington,” Spanberger said.

Spanberger took aim at President Donald Trump’s so-called Big Beautiful Bill saying the effects will impact Virginia and those living here.

From economic concerns over tariffs to the downsizing of the federal workforce, Spanberger said the commonwealth is the second most impacted state.

“That bill will impact the healthcare of hundreds of thousands of Virginians. It will lead to the closure of rural hospitals,” Spanberger said.

Virginia hospitals will lose out on an estimated $26 billion over 14 years under changes to the Medicaid program in the president’s tax and spending law.

Spanberger’s opponent, current lieutenant governor Winsome Earle-Sears, suggested the state cover those cuts with money from the rainy day fund.

12 On Your Side did ask Spanberger what she would do to offset those cuts on the horizon.

“There’s just not an opportunity to use the rainy day fund to contend with what are going to be cuts from the federal government, not just one year but long term into the future,” Spanberger said.

Spanberger says she will also put a focus on affordability, housing and education.

She also wants the state to go from the largest importer of electricity to actually generating it.

“We also need to contend with long term challenges of energy production, energy costs are an issue I hear about across Virginia,” Spanberger said.

State democrats currently hold a slim majority inside the Virginia House of Delegates.

Spanberger would like to see those margins improve since all 100 seats are up for election this year which would make it easier to get a her agenda over the finish line.

“That is what matters. I will endeavor to ensure that even if every single Democrat supports a bill, I want to ensure that there’s greater coalition building beyond just party lines.”

Spanberger also spoke out about President Donald Trump’s high stakes Alaska meeting with Vladimir Putin.

The former CIA officer said the Russian President is an authoritarian dictator, adding the red carpet rollout yielded little results.

“Certainly it’s always a value to try to continue to have negotiations but within the diplomatic space there are always levels of how you handle those,” Spanberger said.

The former congresswoman turned democratic nominee said voters should get to hear from the candidates in at least one debate.

“Fingers crossed for those who enjoy that back and forth,” Spanberger said.

She and her opponent are still at odds over when to square off.

“I’m hoping that we do arrive in having a debate. It looks like that will most likely be the one at Norfolk State,” Spanberger said.

At the moment, Spanberger maintains a double-digit lead over Earle-Sears in recent polling.

It’s a similar situation another democratic nominee for governor found himself in four years ago.

But then, Terry McAuliffe went on to lose against current Governor Glenn Youngkin.

Spanberger said her campaign approach is to run the race as if she is five points behind, adding that they now need to turn the enthusiasm on the campaign trail into actual votes on election day.

“We’re making sure that people aren’t taking anything for granted. I’m certainly not taking anything for granted,” Spanberger said.

Spanberger spent the summer on a bus tour crisscrossing the commonwealth with the rest of the ticket: Lieutenant Governor candidate Ghazala Hashmi and Attorney General candidate Jay Jones.

She’s hoping all the mileage will payoff come November, putting a woman in the commonwealth’s executive chair for the first time.

“It’s going to be a challenging time come January but I’m ready for it,” Spanberger said.

Spanberger also hinted that some start-power would be coming to Virginia in the lead up to Election Day.

That would most likely be in the form of popular governors from other blue states.
