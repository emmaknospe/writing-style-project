By Leila Cox

NORFOLK, Va. — Virginia is now three days away from the November 2025 election, and at the top of the Virginia ballot is the race for governor.

Democratic gubernatorial candidate Abigail Spanberger faces off against current Lieutenant Governor and Republican gubernatorial candidate Winsome Earle-Sears.

Abigail Spanberger and former President Barack Obama rallied with thousands of supporters for the “Virginia Votes Rally” at Chartway Arena Saturday afternoon.

During Obama’s first campaign in 2008, he made one of his taglines the word “hope.”

Today, Democratic leaders called on nearly 7,000 attendees at Chartway Arena to remind Virginians they, too, shape the future of the Commonwealth, and it starts with another four-letter word: “vote.”

“I came out today because I think this is going to be very historical for Abigail,” said Yolanda Stoner. “I was telling people Nov. 4, 2008, was Obama’s day and Nov. 4, 2025, is Abigail’s day.”

Stoner, a Norfolk resident, tells 13New Now why she already cast her ballot for all Democratic candidates across statewide races.

“The message all of them have is a sense of ‘us,'” said Stoner. “And what makes a difference is when somebody focuses on us and the needs of our community, especially the needs of our elderly people and our children…it makes a big difference.”

Democratic candidate for attorney general Jay Jones and Democratic candidate for lieutenant governor Ghazala Hashmi took to the Chartway Arena stage to urge Virginians to vote for elected officials they say put core values over political gain.

“The stakes of this race couldn’t be any higher,” said Jones. ” And the contrast couldn’t be more clear, because we have the opportunity to better course here in Virginia.”

“Lets say it loud and clear, we the people of Virginia are stronger together,” said Hashmi.

Sen. Tim Kaine, Congressman Bobby Scott, Virginia Senate President Pro Tempore Louise Lucas and Virginia House Speaker Don Scott also were in attendance Saturday afternoon, making for a deep slate of Democratic heavy hitters.

“A lot is at stake. That’s why it’s important to vote blue all the way through,” said Congressman Scott.

13News Now heard thousands of attendees shout several chants from “Yes We Can” to “Abby, Abby, Abby” to repeated “Forty-four” chants awaiting former President Barack Obama, the 44th president, to address the crowd.

Before introducing the former president, Spanberger told the crowd she will be a governor who will always put the Commonwealth first.

“Now in Virginia, we need a governor who will recognize that Virginians are struggling to afford the rising costs in healthcare, housing and energy,” said Spanberger.

Spanberger reiterated what she says is her Strengthening Virginia Plan to lower costs for Virginia’s families, grow Virginia’s economy, protect Virginia’s fundamental rights, strengthening Virginia’s schools and delivering for all Virginia families.

“My fellow Virginians, let me be as clear I possibly can be—I will always stand up for Virginia families. I will always work to grow our economy. And I will always put Virginia—not a President, not a party—first,” said Spanberger.

Last month, Obama endorsed Spanberger in her campaign to serve as the next governor of Virginia.

During the former president’s remarks, he highlighted Spanberger’s record. “Abigail doesn’t just say the right thing—as your governor, she will do the right thing,” Obama said.

Obama says not only does Spanberger have a track record of getting the job done, but she does so with enough core conviction to work with both sides of the aisle.

“You’ve got a candidate for governor….somebody who sees you and has been there for you and knows your struggles and will fight for you every single day. Virginia, it is time to point America is a better direction and vote Abigail Spanberger as your next governor,” said Obama.

According to Spanberger’s PR team, Saturday’s rally was the first campaign visit to Hampton Roads of any Democratic President in over a decade.

President Obama last campaigned in Hampton Roads in 2012, when he was running for his own second term.

New data from the Wason Center at Christopher Newport University shows Spanberger with a 7-point lead against Republican challenger Earle-Sears, which is down from a 10-point advantage earlier this month.
