By Richard H. Hronik III

Festival Conference Center at James Madison University was packed Sunday evening as Abigail Spanberger, Democratic candidate for Virginia governor, addressed Democrats from all over the Shenandoah Valley.

Hundreds of people cheered as Spanberger and her running mates, State Senator Ghazala Hashmi, D-Richmond, and Jay Jones, D-Norfolk, took the stage to explain their top priorities if they win the election in November.

Spanberger said that public education and affordable housing would be among her top priorities if she were elected governor.

“We’re going to be in a position where we might be reacting to some of the challenges that we’re in, from a budgetary standpoint,” Spanberger said. “In part, as we are preparing to contend with some of the impacts of the reconciliation bill that was passed into law this past July, but certainly ensuring that we are putting Virginia on a strong path towards passing a wide variety of bills from the General Assembly.”

Spanberger, a former CIA officer, also represented Virginia’s 7th district in Congress until January.

Hashmi, who is running for lieutenant governor alongside Spanberger, has been serving in the state senate since 2019. She was re-elected in 2023 and has since championed reproductive freedom. She has consistently supported legislation that would guarantee access to contraception; however, Gov. Glenn Youngkin has consistently opposed these measures once they reach his desk.

Addressing the crowd, Hashmi said it was time for Virginians to stand up for their rights.

“We absolutely need a governor, a lieutenant governor, and an attorney general who know that it’s more important to Virginians, rather than join that long line of sycophants at Mar-a-Lago,” Hashmi said.

Rounding out the Democratic ticket was Jay Jones. Jones is running to be Virginia’s next attorney general. A former delegate from Norfolk, he said the current Attorney General, Jason Miyares, was failing in his duties to Virginians.

“We spend a lot of time together because the three of us are united in this idea that Virginia deserves better than what we have gotten for the last three-and-a-half years,” Jones said, referring to himself, Hashmi, and Spanberger. “Three people who put Virginia first.”

The democratic candidates for Virginia delegate districts across the Shenandoah Valley also came to address the crowd. Jena Crisler is running against incumbent Chris Runion, R-Bridgewater; Andrew Peyton is challenging Tony Wilt, R-Harrisonburg; and Cathy Rec is running against Justin Pence, R, for the open District 33 seat formerly held by Todd Gilbert.

Peyton said he had a moment of connection with one of his neighbors when he was knocking on doors to find out what was on people’s minds.

“About a week ago, I knocked on the door of a man, about in his 70s,” Peyton said. “I gave him my opening spiel, and it became clear quite quickly that he was having trouble with my English, so I switched to my Spanish, and I did it again. I asked him, as I always do, what some of his top concerns were as he thought about the election this year. He immediately choked up, started to cry, and reached into his pocket to show me his passport. He said he carries it with him everywhere he goes now. I couldn’t really make him any promises in that moment.”

Spanberger and the other Democratic candidates encouraged all present to speak with their neighbors and get everyone they know to vote early, starting on Sept. 19.

“I think the most valuable and most important thing that anyone in politics can do is ensure that, in every single moment, we know that all that it is that we are endeavoring to do is about the people we intend to serve,” Spanberger said. “There is nothing more powerful than asking someone what matters to them.”
