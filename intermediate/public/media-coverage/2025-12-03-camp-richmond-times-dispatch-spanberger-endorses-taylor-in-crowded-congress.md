By Michael Martz

Gov.-elect Abigail Spanberger is endorsing Henrico County Commonwealth’s Attorney Shannon Taylor in a crowded Democratic primary field for the party’s nomination to take on Rep. Rob Wittman, R-1st, in a district that might look different when the time comes to vote.

Fresh off her landslide victory for governor in November, Spanberger announced Tuesday that she is backing Taylor in a shifting Democratic field so crowded that it’s not clear how many candidates remain in the race.

As many as 11 Democrats declared candidacies to take on Wittman, but at least two — Chesterfield County Circuit Court Clerk Amanda Pohl and meteorologist Sean Sublette — have suspended their campaigns.

National Democrats wooed Taylor into the race after her narrow loss in the Democratic primary for attorney general in June. Taylor, serving her fourth term as Henrico commonwealth’s attorney, is the only current elected official in the Democratic contest.

“As Henrico’s top prosecutor, Shannon Taylor has delivered justice with integrity, independence and a deep commitment to the people she serves,” Spanberger said in her endorsement.

“For more than a decade, she has worked to keep families safe — prosecuting violent offenders, protecting victims, and standing firm against those who would do her community harm. Now she’s ready to take that same fight to Washington.”

Spanberger added: “I’ve known Shannon for years, I’ve seen her work up close and I know she will fight tirelessly for the hardworking families of Virginia’s 1st District. I am proud to endorse her campaign and to partner with her to deliver for Virginia.”

Taylor thanked Spanberger for her endorsement and promised to work with her on shared priorities if elected to Congress.

“I look forward to partnering with her to lower costs, keep communities safe and build a Virginia where every family can get ahead,” Taylor said.

The first step for all of the candidates will be determining exactly the district’s boundaries and its voting population. Currently, the 1st District is anchored in Henrico and Chesterfield counties but also encompasses parts of 16 other localities, including Hanover County.

But Democrats who control the General Assembly are poised to redraw the congressional map under a proposed constitutional amendment they passed before Election Day and will attempt to adopt again in January.

Spanberger would then have to act on the resolution to put the proposed amendment on the ballot for voters to decide.

If voters were to agree to amend the constitution, the assembly would redraw the map, bypassing a bipartisan redistricting commission that voters approved in 2020. The commission failed to produce a congressional map and legislative map the next year, leaving it to the Virginia Supreme Court to decide.

Even without redistricting, Wittman’s seat is one of two Republican-held seats that Democrats are targeting after sweeping the top three statewide offices and picking up 13 seats in the House of Delegates.

But Democrats look to pick up more under a new map. Currently, they hold a 6-5 advantage over Republicans in Virginia’s congressional delegation.

The Cook Political Report recently changed its rating for the 1st District from “likely Republican” to “lean Republican.” Cook also rated the 2nd District, based in Virginia Beach and now represented by Rep. Jen Kiggans, R-2nd, as a toss-up instead of “lean Republican.”

Spanberger won the current 1st District by 2 percentage points over Republican Lt. Gov. Winsome Earle-Sears and the 2nd district by 8 points, according to the Virginia Public Access Project.

“There are two seats eminently winnable,” she said in a recent interview with the Richmond Times-Dispatch. “I can say that with firsthand knowledge because I just won them.”
