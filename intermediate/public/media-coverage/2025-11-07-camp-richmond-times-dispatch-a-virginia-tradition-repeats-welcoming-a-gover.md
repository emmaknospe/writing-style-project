By Dave Ress

t’s a Virginia tradition in the days after an election that the outgoing governor shows the governor-elect around the house the newcomer will live in for the next four years.

And with a welcome-to-your-new-home lunch, featuring crabcakes and Virginia wine, Gov. Glenn Youngkin and first lady Suzanne Youngkin did just that Thursday for Gov.-elect Abigail Spanberger and her husband, Adam, before all four came out to the steps of the Executive Mansion on a crisp, blue-sky fall day to exchange final hugs.

“We had a wonderful, wonderful lunch with Governor Youngkin and the first lady. It was a wonderful discussion about the transition process and some of the basic realities of what this is going to mean for our family. So, I’m grateful to the Youngkin family for spending time with us,” Spanberger said.

The governor-elect said they “discussed a whole variety of issues,” including the challenges that any new governor faces with Virginia’s unique-in-the-nation limit of one term in office for its chief executive.

“I think certainly it’s wonderful to have a preceding governor who is committed to making sure that some of those things you didn’t even know to ask questions about are answered,” she said.

Spanberger said first lady Suzanne Youngkin “has been very clear on that front as well, particularly as it relates to the adjustment for our family.”

Spanberger noted that the state constitution requires governors to live at the seat of government, adding “it will be an adjustment for our children, who are all still school-aged kids, and so, that would be an adjustment that we’re working towards.”

“I’m really appreciative” that the governor, the first lady and Youngkin’s team are committed to “ensuring a smooth transition from one administration to the next,” Spanberger said. “This lunch was a lovely way to begin that, and it’s going to be an adventurous few weeks as we head towards January, but a fantastic start.”

Spanberger noted that she’s already got her transition office up and running. She said Tuesday’s election was a mandate for her calls to lower the costs of housing, energy and health care, but warned that there will be challenges because of the impact of Washington policies and legislation.

She defeated Lt. Gov. Winsome Earle-Sears, the Republican nominee, with a margin of 14.5 percentage points, while Democrats flipped 13 House of Delegates seats, boosting their numbers in the 100-member body to 64.

Youngkin said he’ll be working to ensure the change of administration goes smoothly.

“I knew on Tuesday morning, no matter how the day went, I would be standing here ushering in a new governor, and that’s part of Virginia,” he said.

“Virginia has handed a baton from one governor to the next governor for centuries, literally for centuries. And so I think this is part of what makes Virginia special, is that we have an election, the people choose the next governor, and then we go to work to make sure that the outgoing governor hands the incoming governor a state that is in as best possible condition as it possibly can be in.

“There are many things that are still in motion that we will talk about, that hopefully will continue and most importantly, that Virginia can be successful forever,” Youngkin said.

“That’s what this is all about, and that’s why this transition period is one of cooperation (and) is ready to go on Day 1.”
