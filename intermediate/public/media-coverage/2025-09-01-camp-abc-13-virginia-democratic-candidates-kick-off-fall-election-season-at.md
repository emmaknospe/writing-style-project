By Alexia Stanbridge

BUENA VISTA, Va. (WSET) — The Buena Vista Labor Fest marked the official launch of Virginia’s fall election season, drawing prominent candidates from both major parties to engage with voters. The event, a long-standing Labor Day tradition, saw spirited participation from candidates and supporters alike.

The small town setting is a critical stop on the campaign trail. Gubernatorial Candidate Abigail Spanberger and many other big-name democrats spent the day marching in a parade downtown, followed by a dueling rally at Glen Maury Park.

Spanberger emphasized her background in public service during her speech. “I grew up in a house of service. I began my career as a federal agent. I worked narcotics and money laundering cases, and from there I went to the CIA, where I served undercover my whole time focused on fighting terrorism,” Spanberger said.

She outlined her priorities, focusing on reducing the cost of living, housing, healthcare, and energy bills, and stressed the importance of bipartisan support. “Every corner of the Commonwealth, people talk about the cost of living, the cost of housing, the cost of health care, the cost of our energy bills, and I am running to work to lower costs,” she said. She is facing off against the Republican candidate, Winsome Sears.

Spanberger’s supporters echoed the significance of the event. Lisa Tracy said, “We Virginians know that this event in Buena Vista is what kicks off the Virginia races. And that’s why it’s so important for us to be here.” Rene Hasey added, “I do think it is just important to get out and participate in civic life. I mean, my goodness. People can’t be apathetic when it comes to events like this.” Brian McCanon emphasized the need for informed voting, stating, “We need to be informed with facts and not controlled by lies.”

In the race for lieutenant governor, Democratic candidate Ghazala Hashmi highlighted her experience in education and the Virginia State Senate.

“We are going to see so many issues that we, on the state level, have a responsibility to respond to,” Hashmi said. Her priorities include healthcare access, education, reproductive freedom, environmental protection, and voting rights. She is up against Republican candidate John Reid.

For the attorney general position, Democratic candidate Jay Jones called for change in Virginia, advocating for civil rights and healthcare.

“We deserve an attorney general who is going to rise up and fight the rising cost that we see, to make sure that we fight for health care. To make sure that we don’t see rural hospitals close. To make sure that people don’t have to make a choice between life and death because a hospital is too far away,” Jones said. He also highlighted his efforts against ghost gun manufacturers and pledged to serve Virginians as his sole clients if elected.

He is up against incumbent Jason Miyares for attorney general.

The atmosphere at the Labor Fest was electric, with candidates working tirelessly to connect with voters and outline their visions for Virginia’s future.
