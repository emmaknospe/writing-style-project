By Monique Holland

Gubernatorial candidate Abigail Spanberger brought her weeklong Span Virginia Bus Tour to Martinsville on Monday morning.

Spanberger’s tour features more than 40 stops across Virginia’s 11 congressional districts, concluding Saturday.

Spanberger is the Democratic candidate for governor, running with ticket mates state Sen. Ghazala Hashmi for lieutenant governor and former state Del. Jay Jones for attorney general.

Republican Winsome Earle-Sears, currently the lieutenant governor, is her party’s candidate for governor, while John Reid is running for lieutenant governor and current Attorney General Jason Miyares is seeking reelection on the GOP ticket.

The congresswoman served three terms in the U.S. House of Representatives and before that was as a federal law enforcement officer who specialized in working on narcotics and money laundering cases. She also served as a CIA case officer where she worked on counterterrorism and nuclear counterproliferation cases.

Along her tour, Spanberger is sharing her plan to lower costs for all Virginians — her “Affordable Virginia Plan” — which specifically targets lowering the cost of healthcare, housing and energy.

“I am excited to be up here in Martinsville continuing on the bus tour,” she said shortly after the bus parked outside of Be Wiched on Church Street. “My goal is to make sure that I’m introducing myself certainly to Virginians but also importantly having conversations, asking the questions, what issues are important … and ensuring that the people know that I am focused on issues that I so frequently hear about.”

Another of Spanberger’s core values, she said, is strengthening Virginia’s schools.

“In building up our education plan I think there’s a variety of components that are key,” Spanberger said. This includes increasing support for workforce training programs in K-12 schools and community colleges that will lead to good-paying jobs, addressing the cost of high education and getting schools the funding they need while also addressing the teacher shortage.

“As a mother of three daughters in Virginia public schools, my stance is that those dollars should be utilized to make Virginia public schools the best that they can be,” she added. “What we all should be focused on is strengthening our public schools so that every kid across the commonwealth is set up for success as they head off into adulthood — either to continue on their studies or to jump into the workforce.”

Concerning the U.S. involvement on Israel’s war on Iran, Spanberger said, “certainly I have concerns, significant concerns, about the president taking this action without consulting Congress.”

During her time in Congress, Spanberger was one of the House of Representative leaders on issues of authorization of military force.

“The reality is … that there’s potential for use of isolated strikes depending on circumstances and provocations,” she said. “But when we are looking at the potential for continued hostilities and potentially U.S. engagement of U.S. soldiers in a war — that is where Congress has the responsibility to be involved in these discussions.”

“Not necessarily as a check for the president, but frankly it is constitutionally the responsibility of Congress,” she added. “So, it’s important and I hope we see greater communication … hopefully this was an isolated strike.”

After stopping in Martinsville, Spanberger continued her bus tour throughout Southwest Virginia. She said plans to talk to and hear issues from the whole state, letting her meet and hear the concerns of Virginians all the way from Martinsville to Richmond and everywhere in between, she said.

“I’ll continue to physically show up and continue to physically be here to talk to voters about the issues that are important,” Spanberger said. “What have seen and found in my time so far campaigning … is many of the issues are the same, but it’s a variation.”

“Ensuring that I’m showing up, ensuring that I’m showing that I am answering questions, but more importantly just making sure that I know the issues,” she said is important. “It’s a growing region … it is the place where the economy continues to grow and people want to continue to move — so, I think it’s an important, vibrant part of Virginia and I’ll continue to show up.”
