By Sébastien Kraft

On an overcast Friday afternoon, with rain clouds threatening overhead, a large, blue bus pulled into Falls Church’s Eden Center, a Vietnamese-American strip mall known for an abundance of diverse restaurants and small businesses.

Exiting the bus were Abigail Spanberger, the Democratic gubernatorial nominee, and state Sen. Ghazala Hashmi, the newly-minted Democratic nominee for lieutenant governor following a June 17 primary election.

Former Del. Jay Jones, the Democratic nominee for attorney general, was already on site awaiting his running mates’ arrival as the statewide “Span Virginia” bus tour made its way to the Eden Center June 27.

A six-year congresswoman for Virginia’s 7th District from 2019 until January, Spanberger spoke to issues she has encountered on the campaign trail regardless of geographic location.

The Democratic nominee for governor has focused her campaign on affordability through her “Affordable Virginia Plan,” notably with regard to health care, housing and energy costs, efforts to lower prescription drug costs, supporting reproductive freedom and strengthening Virginia’s economy – particularly in the farming and rural sector.

Speaking to InsideNoVa, Spanberger commented on Thursday’s Supreme Court ruling under which Medicaid patients are no longer allowed to freely choose their medical provider – a decision that will allow states to restrict Medicaid funding for Planned Parenthood.

“This morning, I was in Warren County and I had a woman come up to me very concerned about the Supreme Court ruling,” Spanberger said, “noting that for many individuals – women and men – Planned Parenthood is a provider of health care, and that recognition that federal cuts to Medicaid will impact the ability of … so many Virginians and people across the country to be able to potentially receive the health care that they need, very separate and apart from any discussions related to abortion.”

Spanberger said there’s “a kind of lack of understanding of the services provided” at Planned Parenthood, including basic health care and access to contraception.

“Again, totally separate and apart from anything related to abortion,” she said.

Hashmi has also centered her campaign around women’s health care, as well as gun violence prevention, education and job creation – including via “workers’ rights.”

Jones has campaigned on a similar platform, raising issues such as public safety, voting rights, corporate accountability and the right to an abortion.

Joining Spanberger, Hashmi and Jones at Friday’s event were several state lawmakers and other local officials.

Shortly after an initial gathering at the Banh Mi Oi restaurant, the Democratic ticket headed out on a tour of several Eden Center restaurants and shops that culminated in a rally at a venue inside the mall itself.

Speaking to the crowd, Jones cited his previous policy experience.

“I am just a small town boy from Norfolk, Virginia – the son and grandson of civil rights leaders,” Jones said. “When we were in the General Assembly, we expanded civil rights, we increased teacher pay, we raised the minimum wage, we expanded Medicaid – all things that are so deeply important to all of us across this commonwealth.”

Jones continued, “As an assistant attorney general [in the Office of the Attorney General for the District of Columbia], I took on the gun lobby; we went after corporate special interests; we sued landlords; and we protected people all across this country.”

Hashmi, meanwhile, leaned on her 30 years of experience in higher education, notably as an American literature professor.

“I like to say that I was a mild-mannered English professor,” Hasmi said. “There’s nothing really mild-mannered about English professors, right? We’re the ones that are teaching the books that other people are trying to ban.”

Hashmi cited poetry from Walt Whitman and Langston Hughes as inspiration for her initial state Senate campaign in 2019.

“Being here today, it reminds me so much of the lines of Whitman’s poetry,” she said, “where Whitman tries to encompass the full range and breadth of our diverse American identity. And the lovely line that he has is, ‘I hear America singing.’”

Spanberger rounded out the rally with a call for change.

“We need a governor who is going to clearly speak of the impact that bad policy out of D.C. has on Virginians,” Spanberger said. “When we have more than 320,000 federal employees who call Virginia home – some of whom have lost their jobs – and many of whom worry for their friends and neighbors, that’s an impact on their livelihoods. It’s also an impact on our economy and our communities.”

On the Republican side, Winsome Earle-Sears, the Republican gubernatorial nominee, will lead a 6:30 p.m. rally on Tuesday at the Vienna Volunteer Fire Department.

Earle-Sears will be joined by Virginia Gov. Glenn Youngkin, John Reid, the Republican nominee for lieutenant governor, and Jason Miyares, the incumbent attorney general seeking a second term.

Two recent polls have Spanberger out in front. A May 22 Roanoke College poll of 658 Virginia residents saw Spanberger take a 17-point lead over Earle-Sears, 43% to 26%.

Concurrently, another mid-May poll of 1,000 respondents from business organization Virginia FREE showed Spanberger ahead by 4% – including by 6% among independent voters, 53% to 47%.
