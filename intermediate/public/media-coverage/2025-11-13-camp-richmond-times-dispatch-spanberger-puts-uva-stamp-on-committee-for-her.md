By Michael Martz

Gov.-elect Abigail Spanberger put a University of Virginia stamp on her inaugural committee, with two former members of the university’s board of visitors and graduates of its business and medical schools leading a 14-member panel that will plan her inauguration as Virginia’s 75th governor on Jan. 17.

C. Evans Poston Jr., a Darden School of Business graduate and former member of the UVa Board of Visitors who lives in Norfolk, will serve as chairman of the committee. He is director of federal and state government affairs at Troutman Strategies, a law firm affiliate with offices in Richmond and Virginia Beach.

The committee’s co-chairs are Robert and Molly Hardie of Charlottesville, who have strong ties to UVa, Spanberger’s alma mater. Robert Hardie is former rector of the UVa Board of Visitors with three degrees from the university, including master’s and doctoral degrees from the Darden School of Business. Molly Hardie is a graduate of UVa’s School of Medicine and chair of the Thomas Jefferson Foundation Board of Trustees, which operates the former president’s historic home at Monticello in Albemarle County.

Virginians with ties to other state universities also are represented on the inaugural committee. Mehul and Hema Sanghani, of McLean in Fairfax County, are Virginia Tech graduates who donated $10 million to establish the Sanghani Center for Artificial Intelligence and Data Analytics at Tech’s Innovation Campus in Alexandria. He is a former member of the Virginia Tech Board of Visitors and former CEO of Octo, now part of IBM Corp.

Gurpreet “P2” Sandhu, of Manakin Sabot in Goochland County, is a member of the Virginia Commonwealth University Board of Visitors and serves as chief executive officer of The Sandhu Group, a food and hospitality business based in western Henrico County.

Whitney Holt, of Chesterfield County, finance director of Spanberger’s campaign for governor and previous congressional campaigns, will serve as executive director of the inaugural committee.

Spanberger, who won a landslide victory over Republican Lt. Gov. Winsome Earle-Sears, tapped another longtime staff member, Bonnie Krenz-Schnurman, as her chief of staff when she announced her transition team Nov. 5, the day after the election. Karen Mask, her former district office director, will serve as director of the transition team. Krenz-Schnurman and Mask live in Chesterfield County.

The governor-elect did not initially announce that she had asked Kelly Thomasson, executive director of the Dominion Energy Charitable Foundation, to provide administrative support to the transition team. Thomasson, who went to high school with Spanberger at J.R. Tucker in Henrico, is a former secretary of the commonwealth under Govs. Ralph Northam and Terry McAuliffe. She lives in Alexandria with her husband, Clark Mercer, who served as chief of staff to Northam and now is executive director of the Metropolitan Council of Governments.

Members of the inauguration committee also include Jeffrey Breit, co-founder and partner of a Virginia Beach law firm, in which House Speaker Don Scott, D-Portsmouth, is a partner.

Locally, the panel includes Mark Bowles, chairman of McGuireWoods Consulting, a high-powered affiliate of Virginia’s largest law firm, and Adisa Muse, government affairs director at Hunton Andrews Kurth, the state’s second largest law firm.

The committee also includes Pam Royall, who is research director at EAB, formerly Royall & Co., in Richmond. She is former chair of the board of trustees at the Virginia Museum of History and Culture, on which she still serves.

The panel features two members with deep international backgrounds. Kellie Meiman Hock of Arlington County is an adjunct professor at Georgetown University, a former foreign service officer in Latin America and former director at the U.S. Trade Representative for Brazil and the Southern Cone in South America. Dana Shell Smith, also of Arlington, is former U.S. Ambassador to Qatar and longtime public affairs official at the State Department, which she left in 2017 during President Donald Trump’s first term.

Other members include Warren Thompson of Fairfax, president and CEO of the Thompson Hospitality Corp., the largest Black-led food services and facilities management company in the U.S., and Jan Brandt of Fairfax, former vice chair and chief marketing officer at America Online, or AOL.
