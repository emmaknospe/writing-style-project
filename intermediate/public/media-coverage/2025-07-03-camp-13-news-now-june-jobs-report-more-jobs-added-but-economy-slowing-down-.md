By Madison Schlegel

NORFOLK, Va. — Bob McNab, chair of the Department of Economics at Old Dominion University, said the latest jobs report from June shows a rise in the job market.

He emphasizes that you have to look at the types of jobs added compared to the number of people who have seen layoffs this year.

“The U.S economy added more than 140,000 jobs, well above expectations,” McNab explained. “Well, that’s because state and local governments added 80,000 jobs. The private sector only added about 74,000 jobs, well below expectations.”

McNab said these numbers from the U.S Bureau of Labor Statistics show how slow the job market is moving. He expects that trend to continue through the year, mainly because federal employees who’ve taken offers to leave federal positions are still counted as employed until this fall.

Come October 1, McNab said all of those people are going to show up as unemployed across Hampton Roads and Virginia.

“We should see unemployment increase in the fall, just from what’s happening with the federal government,” he explained. “And that doesn’t include the uncertainty surrounding tariffs.”

McNab said the next struggle is the pay difference for people who have been laid off and are looking to find new employment.

“A federal government employee earns about 1.6 times that of the average private sector employee. So, yes, there are private sector jobs out there, but on average they don’t pay the same amount as civilian public employment,” said McNab.

Democratic candidate for Virginia governor, Abigail Spanberger, previously told 13News Now she’s concerned about unemployment rates.

“The reality is the impact on Virginia’s economy is likely to be dire, because it isn’t just the loss of jobs and the loss of livelihood for those individuals who have lost their jobs, but it’s the government contractors,” she said during a campaign stop last weekend.

Meanwhile, Republican gubernatorial candidate Winsome Earle-Sears has come out behind President Donald Trump and the recent federal layoffs. In March, Earle-Sears commented to reporters about how she doesn’t want people to lose their jobs, but said layoffs are not unusual.

We reached out to the Earle-Sears campaign for a more recent comment about the new unemployment numbers, but they have not responded at this time.
