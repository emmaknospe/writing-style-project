By Kate Nuechterlein

CHARLOTTESVILLE, Va. (WVIR) – Even in the middle of a heat wave, Eastwood Farm and Winery in Albemarle County was packed on Tuesday night, June 24, with voters awaiting Virginia’s Democratic nominee for governor: Abigail Spanberger.

As part of the “Span Virginia” bus tour with more than forty stops across the Commonwealth, Spanberger outlined her vision for the Governor’s Mansion to a crowd of hundreds in Albemarle County.

She was joined by State Senator Ghazala Hashmi, the Democratic nominee for Lieutenant Governor, and former Delegate Jay Jones, the Democratic nominee for Attorney General.

“This ticket is special,” Jones said, amid cheers from the crowd.

The three nominees stood side by side on stage, just one week after the Democratic primaries.

“Everybody sees that we are united, we’re committed to fight,” Hashmi said.

Spanberger says the three have spent the last seven days discussing their legislative priorities and leaning on each other’s different areas of expertise.

“It’s really exciting to know what our ticket is and to be campaigning together,” Spanberger said.

On the anniversary of the Supreme Court’s decision to overturn Roe v. Wade, the nominees focused their campaign message around abortion rights. With speeches from members of Planned Parenthood, the rally reminded the crowd that Virginia is the only state in the South that has not restricted abortion access in the aftermath of the Dobbs decision and called attention to the constitutional amendment that would enshrine reproductive rights in Virginia’s Constitution.

Hashmi herself opened up to the crowd about her own experience with miscarriages.

“I could have hemorrhaged to death, I could’ve suffered septic shock, but my doctor knew that she could make the best medical decision for me,” Hashmi said. “This is what we’re fighting for.”

Spanberger later told 29News that Tuesday’s rally was the first time she ever heard Hashmi’s story.

“It was pretty brave of her and courageous of her to share that story and it’s one more reason why I’m proud to have her as a ticket mate,” Spanberger said.

Across central Virginia, the room was filled with supporters eager to voice why they’re voting for Spanberger.

“I absolutely love her platform about protecting women’s rights, and that is something I’m absolutely huge about,” said Savannah Peterson, a young woman who lives in Fluvanna County. “It’s so inspiring to see her come out and fight.”

David Bateman, who lives in Charlottesville, says his number one priority ahead of November is protecting and expanding public education. He says he thinks Spanberger will do just that, calling the stakes of the election “huge.”

“Her record in Congress and her bills that she’s supported in Congress when she was there, all support that she wants to make sure that all kids receive an education, not just few,” Bateman said.

It was the energy on Tuesday night that Democrats are hoping they can keep riding until the polls open in the fall. Not just for statewide office, but for the House of Delegates, which is also up in 2025.

“We currently have 51 people within the house of delegates who voted to enshrine the right to an abortion in the constitution, and after this election, we need to ensure that we retain those 51 members or increase their margins,” Spanberger said.

The Republican ticket – gubernatorial nominee and current Lieutenant Governor Winsome Earle-Sears, nominee for Lieutenant Governor John Reid, and incumbent nominee for Attorney General Jason Miyares- will be speaking together in public for the first time at a rally in Vienna, Virginia on July 1st.
