By Hannah Eason Amado

Virginia’s Democratic lawmakers and healthcare advocates are pushing back against the U.S. Senate’s passage of President Donald Trump’s “One Big Beautiful Bill Act.”

The Trump administration touted the passage of the massive spending plan on Tuesday, claiming the legislation lowers taxes, eliminates waste and fraud from the Medicaid system, revolutionizes the nation’s defense, and strengthens border security.

The bill now goes back to the House, where GOP leaders aim to pass it by July 4th.

Virginia Sens. Mark Warner and Tim Kaine released a joint statement after voting against the bill, stating, “Americans deserve better than this.”

“Today, Republicans jammed through a partisan megabill that slashes Medicaid, nutrition assistance, and other critical programs that Americans rely on in order to pay for massive tax breaks to the very rich,” the Democratic senators said. “It’s clear that the Trump Administration and congressional Republicans are only interested in helping the wealthiest—even if it means ripping off working- and middle-class people, killing jobs, and hurting our economy.”

Both Warner and Kaine filed more than a dozen amendments to the bill, including a move to rename the legislation to the “Hurt People, Kill Jobs, and Spike the Debt to Reward the Rich Act.”

Kaine’s amendments, which were rejected, centered on protecting Medicaid and SNAP benefits, providing assistance to small businesses and farmers struggling with trade impacts, and restoring funding to the Building Resilient Infrastructure and Communities grant program, which included a $24 million grant to enhance the Lake Meade Dam in Portsmouth.

Rep. Abigail Spanberger, D-Virginia, said the bill threatens to take away healthcare coverage from thousands of Virginians and drive up insurance costs.

“The legislation passed in the U.S. Senate today would rip away healthcare coverage from hundreds of thousands of our neighbors, including Virginians with disabilities,” Spanberger said.

Roughly 16 million people — including more than 300,000 Virginians — could lose health insurance by 2034 under cuts proposed by Trump’s administration, according to an analysis by the Joint Economic Committee – Minority. Per the report, about 136,000 Virginians could lose Affordable Care Act coverage, while another 166,000 could lose Medicaid coverage.

The House version of the bill creates work requirements for many able-bodied enrollees under the age of 65. They would be required they work, volunteer, or go to school in exchange for Medicaid coverage. The Senate version of the bill proposes steeper cuts to Medicaid, Medicare and the Affordable Care Act.

Spanberger also expressed concern about the potential impact on Virginia hospitals.

“This bill would threaten the survival of our rural hospitals, overwhelm our emergency rooms as more Virginians forego preventative care and check-ups, and drive up costs for Virginia families,” Spanberger said.

In a letter sent in June to Trump, Majority Leader John Thune and House Speaker Mike Johnson, six hospitals in Virginia were listed as being at-risk for closure. The letter, penned by four Democratic senators, defined at-risk hospitals as operating in the top 10% of Medicaid payer mix among rural U.S. hospitals, and the hospital had experienced three consecutive years of negative total margin.

Virginia Hospital & Healthcare Association said the plan includes “severe” Medicaid funding cuts. The organization said the Senate’s version of the legislation “would cost Virginia hospitals more than $2 billion annually in essential funding.”

“The effect of that financial impact would be devastating to hospitals,” a statement from the association said. “It is likely that several hospitals, including those serving rural parts of Virginia, could end up closing in that scenario.”

The organization, which represents 26 health systems across the commonwealth, says the House version of the bill “is the better approach to accomplish the President’s tax-cutting objectives while still preserving our health care delivery system and health safety net.”
