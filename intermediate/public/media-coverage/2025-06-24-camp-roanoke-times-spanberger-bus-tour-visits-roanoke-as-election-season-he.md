By Luke Weir

Democratic gubernatorial candidate Abigail Spanberger parked her campaign bus in Roanoke on Monday evening and offered her party’s ticket for state offices as a safeguard against “chaos coming out of Washington.”

“We turn on the news and it’s one difficult story after next,” Spanberger said. “We have to let it fuel us. We have to say, ‘I do not accept what I am seeing, and therefore I will work to change it.’”

Spanberger, a former CIA officer and a U.S. representative for Virginia from 2019 until her run for governor, spoke to a crowd of supporters in the Virginia Museum of Transportation rail yard.

“We need a governor who will stand up for Virginians and make clear that the attacks coming out of Washington hurt our communities,” Spanberger said. “Importantly, we also need a governor who has a vision for what comes after this time of division. We need a governor who is focused on our communities, not just sowing chaos.”

On the Republican side, Lt. Gov. Winsome Earle-Sears is running for governor to replace Gov. Glenn Youngkin, since Virginia governors can only serve one consecutive term. Whichever gubernatorial candidate is successful will become the first woman elected governor in Virginia history.

Spanberger has been touring Virginia by bus, including earlier stops Monday in the New River Valley, as the campaign for governor ramps up toward the November election. She said she has heard concerns from residents about housing affordability, cuts to Medicaid, attacks on education, and the most-hated tax in Virginia, the car tax.

“The biggest driver for me getting in this race is a desire to ensure that Virginia is a place of opportunity for all of our families and all of our kids,” Spanberger said. “That comes with ensuring our education system here is the best in the nation, and it is putting us on a path toward contending with the issues of teacher shortages.”

She was joined in Roanoke on Monday by state Sen. Ghazala Hashmi, D-Chesterfield, the party’s candidate for lieutenant governor, who said Virginia is ready to “send a loud message” to Washington during this first statewide election after the 2024 presidential contest. Hashmi is running against Richmond radio show host John Reid, the Republican candidate.

“We have to have state-level leadership that is ready to protect all Virginians,” Hashmi said. “That is ready to join that coalition of Democratic leadership — governors, attorney generals and lieutenant governors — who are forming the new brick wall to stand up to the chaos that’s happening in Washington.”

Donna Littlepage is the Democratic candidate for House District 40 in the Virginia legislature, challenging incumbent Rep. Joe McNamara, R-Salem. She said her past experience balancing Carilion Clinic’s billion-dollar budget would be an asset in the Capitol.

“I saw the changes happening to Medicaid in Washington, as well as the other safety net programs like SNAP, and just didn’t feel like I could sit on the sidelines,” Littlepage said. “I do believe if we work together, we can make good things come.”

Roanoke Councilman Peter Volosin said the city has been affected by federal-level cuts.

“They got rid of $13 million of federal research money that goes to … Fralin Biomedical Research Institute,” Volosin said. “Our future is in biotech.”

After primary elections last week, now begins a five-month sprint for candidates campaigning to the Nov. 5 general election. But there are only about three months until early voting starts Sept. 19.
