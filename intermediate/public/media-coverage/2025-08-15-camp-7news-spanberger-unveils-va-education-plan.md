By Princess Harrell

PORTSMOUTH, Va. (7News) — As Virginia students return to class, education is defining the governor’s race, with Democrat Abigail Spanberger detailing her “Strengthening Virginia Schools” education plan, if elected governor, and Republicans highlighting discipline and parental choice.

Shauneille Perry-Wallace, a middle school counselor and Portsmouth Public Schools’ 2024 Middle School Teacher of the Year, spoke last Friday in support of Spanberger’s plan, saying, “Public education is the great equalizer.”

Princess Moss, a Louisa County teacher and vice president of the National Education Association, added: “She listens, she acts, and she will deliver for Virginia’s families.”

Both educators back Spanberger’s proposals, which call for new literacy and math curriculums, recruiting federal employees and servicemembers into teaching, and expanding statewide background checks for school staff to help keep students safe.

“Educators are burning out, and many students are being left behind. Not because of lack of potential, but because we lack the resources to support them. We need leaders who understand that investing in public education isn’t optional. It is essential,” Perry-Wallace said.

Spanberger said she wants schools to have “high-quality instructional materials,” adding, “We have fallen behind, but I will make sure Virginia students have the opportunity to excel, and I will make sure our schools have access to high-quality instructional materials proven to challenge students and also set them up for success.”

Recent national test results and the Education Recovery Scorecard show Virginia students continue to struggle in reading and math.

Perry-Wallace said schools face “teacher shortages, outdated facilities, growing class sizes, and increasing mental health needs among students, all while being asked to do more with less.”

Gov. Glenn Youngkin’s K-12 accountability system rates schools from “Distinguished” to “Needs Intensive Support” based on student success. Critics said it doesn’t accurately show how schools are performing, or do enough to help schools that come up short.

Youngkin has pushed back, saying that schools have seen a record increase of 40% in funding per student since he came into office.

Spanberger said she would review the system if elected.

“I look forward to that JLARC study to best understand what this new accountability system will deliver as a function of what it communicates back to parents, to teachers, to school districts. And certainly I looked forward to strengthening what is found to be working and advantageous and improving what might have shortcomings.”

JLARC stands for Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission. It is a legislative body that oversees state agencies and programs through evaluations, policy analyses, and performance audits to ensure effectiveness, efficiency, and proper use of public funds. Their findings for Virginia’s accountability system are set to be completed in December.

Spanberger rolled out her education plan with Democratic running mates: Sen. Ghazala Hashmi for lieutenant governor and Del. Jay Jones for attorney general.

Jones, born in Norfolk, cited his family’s ties to public education in Virginia.

“My family only exists here today because of the teachers’ union. When my grandfather, Hilary, passed away at age 52 in an untimely manner, my grandmother, Corinne, got a job. and she became a middle school teacher at Norview Middle School, supporting my father and 2 uncles on a teacher’s salary.”

He added, “As a delegate, we increased teacher pay, because we always support our teachers and as an assistant attorney general, we got guns off the street to make our communities safe and make our schools safe as well.”

Spanberger’s platform also includes affordable early childhood education and investments in Virginia’s public colleges.
