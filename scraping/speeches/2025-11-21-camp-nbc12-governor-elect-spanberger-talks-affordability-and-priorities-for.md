By Henry Graff

RICHMOND, Va. (WWBT) – Virginia Governor-elect Abigail Spanberger is getting personal about her newfound life, the transfer of power and new priorities.

“For my family, it’s been interesting so far and we’re getting used to what this new step will mean,” said Spanberger.

The incoming governor spoke with 12 On Your Side for her first television sit down interview.

With the transfer of power underway, Spanberger is focused on following through on campaign promises.

That includes tackling affordability concerns by bringing down costs.

“Housing prices have gone up. The cost of renting has gone up 14%, 18% to buy and there’s not one single switch to flip and create more supply or bring down costs and so it will be a long term and very, very targeted focus of my administration to ensure that every lever we can pull,” said Spanberger.

The Democrat also says there’s too much gray area around marijuana right now due to the lack of a retail marketplace.

She would support legislation that makes it clear what people are buying.

“Also, that the revenues coming in are put predominantly towards education and so I think I’ve been clear with members of the general assembly, and I haven’t seen or encountered any major differences,” said Spanberger.

This week, state lawmakers began filing legislation for the upcoming Virginia General Assembly session.

That includes three constitutional amendments and a redistricting push, among others.

State Republicans are already accusing Democrats of not following through on a campaign promise to lower costs.

When asked, Spanberger pointed to a bill set to raise the minimum wage, adding there will be much more on that front.

“Then importantly, some of the proposals that I put out along the campaign, we’re still working to get the legislation written and ultimately introduced. And I’m glad that affordability is a priority of so many of the republican members of the general assembly,” said Spanberger.

12 On Your Side also pressed Spanberger about what executive actions she would take on day one, after being sworn in.

“Looking towards initial executive orders, for the first day, the kind of meat of what I’m looking at is how we can have an impact on the everyday experience of Virginians. So more to come on that,” said Spanberger.

Spanberger is against a proposal to repeal the state’s right to work law introduced by another Democratic lawmaker.

“You know she is well aware. We’ve discussed the fact that I said that I will not sign that bill but she wants to have that conversation and that’s her prerogative,” said Spanberger.

As for the dreaded car tax, don’t think that’s going away on day one.

It would take a constitutional amendment to repeal the tax itself and then lawmakers need to find a new source of revenue.

“It becomes part of a longer conversation of if and when we are able to remove the car tax, how do we ensure that that revenue that pays for schools, pays for local police, local law enforcement that it is replaced,” said Spanberger.

Spanberger also says she hasn’t received an invitation to meet with President Donald Trump at the White House.

And if one comes, she would likely go.

“So if there is an invitation and if there is something I can work on with this president, just I have had during my time in Congress, worked with a Democratic president, a Republican president, I will do it,” said Spanberger.

Spanberger will be sworn in as the 75th governor of the Commonwealth of Virginia on Jan. 17.
