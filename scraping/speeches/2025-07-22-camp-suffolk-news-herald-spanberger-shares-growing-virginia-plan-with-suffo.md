By Avery Goodstine

Democratic Governor nominee Abigail Spanberger stopped at Suffolk’s Tidewater Community College Campus on Tuesday afternoon to speak about her Growing Virginia Plan. The plan outlines how Spanberger will work to grow Virginia’s workforce, business investment, and trade opportunities to make the Commonwealth the best for business.

Speaking in support of Spanberger were Delegate Nadarius Clark (D) representing the 84th district and Bert Dodson, Jr., president and CEO of Dodson Pest Control. Elected officials from Norfolk, Portsmouth, and Virginia Beach were also in attendance.

A recent Bureau of Labor Statistics report stated Virginia was the only state to see a statistically significant increase in unemployment rates from May to June, going from 3.4% to 3.5%. This increase marks the sixth consecutive increase in unemployment in the Commonwealth.

Because of this, Spanberger said it’s more important now than ever that Virginian voters know she will be “focused relentlessly on growing our economy, our workforce, and increasing investment and trade opportunities for Virginia” if elected Governor.

“Day one, this starts with having a Governor who is not afraid to say that the threats to the jobs of hundreds of 1000s of Virginians is a threat to Virginia’s economy,” Spanberger said.

On her campaign trail, Spanberger said Virginians’ priorities across the state focus on the same things: increasing opportunities for themselves, for their children, and for their communities.

“But to do that, to grow Virginia’s economy and to connect workers with new opportunities, Virginians first need to actually be able to afford to live in our Commonwealth,” she said. “And over the last few months, I’ve announced several key priorities focused on lowering costs. We’re calling it our Affordable Virginia Plan.”

She mentioned previously speaking about her plans to lower the cost of prescription and healthcare drugs, lower the cost of housing for first time buyers and renters, and to lower energy bills.

She said the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) is threatening Virginia’s economy with job loss, and local business owners and contractors are worried about how their customer base will be affected.

She said the increased tariffs are straining local farmers and producers and creating uncertainty at the Port of Virginia.

“These tariffs are putting pressure on families who are already feeling more strain on their wallets,” she said. “We need a Governor who will not dismiss these threats to our economy, but one who actually has a plan to rebuild a more resilient economy.”

When speaking on federal cuts leading to job loss, Spanberger said she recognizes the important work those federal workers do, and, if elected, she will ensure there is an opportunity for them to transition into the private sector, state public service jobs, or elsewhere in the federal government.

A large part of Spanberger’s Growing Virginia Plan involves preparing younger generations to enter the workforce. She plans to increase awareness of apprenticeships, internships, and work-based learning opportunities; expand opportunities for high school students to earn college credits, and equip middle and high school students with skills needed for high-demand jobs.

She plans to align education and workforce training initiatives with the needs of businesses across Virginia in ways like promoting programs in clean energy and shipbuilding.

As Governor, Spanberger said she will continue to “make the business case” for renewable energy. She’s proud that Hampton Roads will be home to the largest offshore wind installation in the country and speaks to the potential renewable energy has in Virginia.

Additionally, she wants Virginia to set an example for how renewable energy can create a local pipeline of jobs.

She said her administration will address workforce shortages in healthcare, education, and manufacturing, helping to remove barriers to entry in these jobs and others.

“If Virginia’s leaders support our talent here at home, our skilled workforce will continue to drive growth within the Commonwealth and attract private investment in major employers to Virginia,” Spanberger said, “because businesses will know that Virginians are prepared to fill vacancies in cutting edge industries like advanced manufacturing and cybersecurity. But Virginia needs more than a skilled and prepared workforce to attract and grow investments. We need a leader who understands what brings new businesses to Virginia and how we can help promote growth in Virginia.”

She continued to highlight affordable childcare, reliable infrastructure, water systems, internet, housing, public schools, and healthcare as areas she will focus on improving.

Spanberger has a goal to make Virginia the top state for business, which she will do by fostering a competitive business climate, making investments that will support tourism, and make it easier for small businesses to start and grow.

“Come January, Virginia will have a Governor who is laser focused on lowering costs for Virginia families and building an economy that brings new opportunities to Virginia’s workers, Virginia’s businesses, and Virginia’s communities,” she said.
