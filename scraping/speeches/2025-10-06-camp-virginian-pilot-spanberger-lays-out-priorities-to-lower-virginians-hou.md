Spanberger: The Commonwealth “Has a Clear Role to Play in Bringing Down Housing Costs”

Virginian-Pilot: Earle-Sears “Did Not Respond to Several Requests for an Interview or to Answer Questions About Housing Policies She Supports. Her Website Does Not Reference a Housing Plan”

RICHMOND, Va. — New reporting from the Virginian-Pilot underscores Congresswoman Abigail Spanberger’s focus on lower housing costs for both Virginia renters and buyers — outlined in her Affordable Virginia Plan.

Recent polling shows more than 70 percent of Virginians believe there is not enough housing available in the Commonwealth that they can afford. While Spanberger has a concrete plan to save Virginians money and help families build a life here in the Commonwealth, Winsome Earle-Sears “did not respond to several requests for an interview or to answer questions about housing policies” and has no public plan to lower housing costs.

Click here to read the Virginian-Pilot highlight the contrast between the two candidates, or read excerpts below.

—

Spanberger, the Democratic candidate, lists housing as a component of her Affordable Virginia plan, which also includes healthcare and energy policies.

“‘These tools range from contributions to local housing trust funds to incentivizing development near public transit to making it easier to convert unused commercial space into housing,’ she said in a statement. ‘But the Commonwealth also has a clear role to play in bringing down housing costs — and come January, I will work in close collaboration with our state agencies to cut red tape and streamline state review and permitting processes that slow down construction and drive up housing costs for Virginians.’”

“Other policy proposals in Spanberger’s housing plan include incentives for construction of starter homes or smaller homes for first time homeowners, encouraging the redevelopment of abandoned commercial buildings for residential use, increasing funding to the Virginia Housing Trust Fund for affordable housing, and re-establishing an Interagency Council on Homelessness.”

“Spanberger’s plan also calls for giving localities a transferrable right of first refusal to buy public supported housing — when affordable housing comes up for sale, or the time that it is contracted to be affordable expires, localities or other qualified groups would get first crack at buying that property with the intention of preserving it as affordable housing.”

BACKGROUND

Earlier this year, Spanberger rolled out her plan to lower costs for Virginians — her Affordable Virginia Plan, which is focused on lowering healthcare, housing, and energy costs for Virginia families.

Spanberger has also announced her Growing Virginia Plan and Strengthening Virginia Schools Plan — showing that her administration will have a plan on day one to address challenges facing Virginia’s economy and schools.

As a Member of Congress, Spanberger built a record of working with both Democrats and Republicans to deliver results for Virginians — and was named the most bipartisan Member of Congress from Virginia.
