By Ben Swenson

JAMES CITY — The Democratic nominees for statewide office stopped near Williamsburg on Saturday as part of an eight-day, 40-stop campaign tour of Virginia.

Abigail Spanberger, candidate for governor; state Sen. Ghazala Hashmi, who is vying to be the commonwealth’s next lieutenant governor; and Jerrauld “Jay” Jones, who is running for attorney general, were joined by Jessica Anderson, who is seeking to represent Virginia’s House of Delegates District 71.

The event, hosted by the Historic Triangle Democrats at Lafayette High School in James City County, drew approximately 400 attendees.

The campaign rally kicked off the final day of the Span Virginia Bus Tour, which visited every region of the state, aiming to mobilize voters to support the Democratic ticket. Spanberger, Hashmi and Jones moved on to their last stops in Newport News, Norfolk and Virginia Beach after leaving James City.

Prior to addressing attendees, Spanberger said she looked forward to talking with voters in the Williamsburg area about issues such as the affordability of housing, energy and health care, the importance of supporting education and what she said was the “reality of chaos on the ground” caused by President Donald Trump and Gov. Glenn Youngkin’s acquiescence to his agenda.

Spanberger, an alumna of the University of Virginia, called Friday’s resignation of university President James Ryan under pressure from the Trump administration “an attack on academic freedom.” She also said she worries about the “chilling effects” of recent arrests by Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents of immigrants at the Chesterfield County Courthouse this week. At least 14 immigrants had been arrested by ICE agents in and around the courthouse in the past week.

Inside Lafayette High School’s cafeteria, Anderson introduced Jones and said that the trio of statewide candidates onstage would be the best hope to enact policies that would improve the lives of Virginians.

Jones, a native of Norfolk and a graduate of William & Mary, spoke fondly of the statewide bus tour and took a swipe at the Republican slate of statewide candidates, which includes lieutenant governor hopeful John Reid, whom Youngkin asked to withdraw from the race following revelations he may have posted explicit messages online — a claim that Reid denied.

“We actually like each other,” Jones said. “We actually talk to each other. We ride on a big blue bus together.”

Jones highlighted his own record in the House of Delegates standing up to the gun lobby and special interests and listed what he said are the shortcomings of Republican Attorney General Jason Miyares, who is running for another term in office.

Hashmi said that as an immigrant and a Muslim — she was born in India and came to the United States as a child — she felt compelled to run for lieutenant governor to stand up for marginalized Virginians whose voices are seldom heard.

“One thing we know is that Democrats are fighters,” she said.

Hashmi touted her record as a state senator and said people sometimes describe her as a mild-mannered English teacher, as she spent almost 30 years in higher education.

“If you know anything about English teachers, we’re not mild-mannered,” she said. “We’re actually the people who are teaching the books that other people are trying to ban.”

Spanberger told the crowd that she hears from Virginians about what she called the “assault” on the workforce, health care, food security, research dollars and the economy. She said that there are “new motivations” daily that propel her campaign forward.

“When there are more than 320,000 Virginians whose jobs are under threat by the current administration, we need a governor of Virginia to stand up for those individuals, for their service and importantly for the strength of Virginia’s economy,” she said.

After the candidates spoke, Anderson said conditions were favorable for a wave of Democratic victories in November, including in her own district and in statewide offices, because Republican policies were so deeply unpopular.

“This is like 2017 on steroids,” Anderson said, referring to the year following Trump’s first inauguration.

Anderson said she is regularly knocking on doors in the district and frequently hears concerns about affordability of health care and everyday expenses — costs she said will increase under current and proposed policies.

Among the supporters who came out to support the Democratic candidates was James City County resident Denise Harrington. She said that as a grandparent and retired teacher, she is concerned about the direction Virginia is headed. Harrington indicated that she wanted to support politicians who are truthful and could restore hope and respect to Virginia.

“We can’t take Virginia back to the days of the Dixiecrats,” she said.
