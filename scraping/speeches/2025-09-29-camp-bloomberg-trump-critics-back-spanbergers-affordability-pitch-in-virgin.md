By Skylar Woodhouse & Mario Parker

The Cost Coalition, a bipartisan group of Donald Trump adversaries, is throwing its support behind Virginia Democratic gubernatorial candidate Abigail Spanberger, who has largely centered her campaign pitch to voters on addressing worries over affordability.

“For Virginia families concerned with rising cost of living, the choice in the November election is clear: Abigail Spanberger is a proven leader that is committed to fighting to lower costs,” Cost Coalition advisers Terry Holt, a Republican, and Andrew Bates, a Democrat, said in a statement. “Spanberger put Virginia’s working families first by opposing the Trump Administration’s tariffs that are raising prices and worsening inflation.”

The group comprises both Republicans and Democrats, including Holt, a onetime George W. Bush spokesperson, and Bates, who was a spokesperson in former President Joe Biden’s White House.

The Virginia governor’s election between Spanberger, a former US representative, and Republican Winsome Earle-Sears, currently the state’s lieutenant governor, is scheduled for Nov. 4. The off-year election has historically served as an early bellwether for the national midterms that take place a year later and determine control of Congress.

Democrats have been smarting for the better part of the year after Trump in 2024 swept swing states and defeated former Vice President Kamala Harris to take the White House and Republicans retained control of the US House and won the US Senate. But Democrats have since landed on a message of lowering the cost of living, seizing on Trump’s implementation of tariffs on US trading partners and key sectors of the economy, levies that most mainstream economists warn threaten to spike prices for businesses and consumers.

The core inflation rate is at 2.9%, higher than the Federal Reserve’s target of 2%. Several policymakers, including Fed Chair Jerome Powell, acknowledge the impact of tariffs on prices has so far been muted, though they’ve taken a cautious approach to interest rate cuts due to lingering uncertainty about the trajectory of inflation and the economy.

Democrats are also seeking to highlight the Trump administration’s sweeping layoffs of federal employees since January, an issue which has a particular resonance in Virginia, a state bordering Washington, D.C. and home to many government workers.

Republican Virginia Governor Glenn Youngkin won the state’s 2021 gubernatorial race — retaking the governor’s mansion after Democrats won the previous two contests — but is term-limited. Youngkin won his election by casting himself as a moderate Republican, at times keeping Trump at arm’s length. Earl Sears, has taken a different approach on the campaign trail, leaning into conservative policies on crime, immigration and transgender students.

The Earle-Sears campaign did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

“With Washington’s tariffs, tax cuts for the rich, and chaos making everyday life unaffordable, Virginia voters have an opportunity to send a message that there is a better way by electing a governor committed to lowering costs and making Virginia an even better place to do business and raise a family,” Holt and Bates said.

Spanberger leads Earle-Sears by 52% to 40% among likely voters, according to a poll released earlier this month by Christopher Newport University’s Wason Center for Civic Leadership. Key issues for Virginia voters include inflation, which 21% of poll respondents cited, threats to democracy at 18%, K-12 education at 9% and immigration and crime at 9% each.

Virginia’s race is not the only high-profile gubernatorial race in November, with New Jersey featuring a contest between Democratic US Representative Mikie Sherrill and Republican Jack Ciattarelli.
