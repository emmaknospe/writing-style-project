In Congress, Spanberger Successfully Led the Bipartisan Effort to Recognize America’s Atomic Veterans Each Year on July 16

RICHMOND, Va. — Congresswoman Abigail Spanberger today recognized National Atomic Veterans Day 2025 and reaffirmed her commitment to making sure all of America’s heroes receive the recognition they deserve.

In December 2021, the President signed into law Spanberger’s bipartisan legislation to recognize America’s Atomic Veterans every year on July 16. Spanberger’s law requires the President to issue a proclamation every year to observe National Atomic Veterans Day.

“Today marks the 80th anniversary of the 1945 Trinity detonation in New Mexico — the world’s first detonation of a nuclear device. In the years that followed, thousands of patriotic Virginians and Americans from across our country who left our shores to protect our American ideals were exposed to dangerous levels of toxic radiation during nuclear testing. These heroes are known as America’s ‘Atomic Veterans.’

“Throughout their service and the following decades, Atomic Veterans swore an oath of secrecy. These patriots were banned from confiding in their loved ones or sharing the extent of their service with their doctors. Because they were unable to share their stories, too many Veterans were denied the recognition, benefits, and medical care they earned through their service. And for decades, the American public was kept in the dark.

“As a Member of Congress, I was honored to lead the charge to make sure these selfless heroes finally received recognition for the full extent of their service to our country. The annual recognition of ‘National Atomic Veterans Day’ gives an opportunity to reflect on Atomic Veterans’ sacrifices and valor, express our deep gratitude to their families, and make sure Virginians and Americans across our country continue to understand the critical role they played in keeping our nation safe.”

In Congress, Spanberger worked alongside Veterans, military families, and advocacy organizations to finally secure Atomic Veterans and their loved ones the annual recognition they deserve.

“One of the many traits about Abigail Spanberger that I admired the most during her time in Congress was a willingness to not only listen to her constituents, but to introduce meaningful legislation that addressed their concerns,” said Steve Robertson, former Legislative Director, The American Legion and former Majority Staff Director, U.S. Senate Veterans’ Affairs Committee. “The National Atomic Veterans Day legislation pays tribute to the countless number of World War II veterans who were exposed to the aftermath of both the dropping of the first bombs in Japan, as well as the numerous testing programs within the United States. Most of the Atomic Veterans are no longer with us, but Americans should always remember their service and sacrifice. Their special day of recognition is well overdue.”

Robertson continued, “I also believe that Spanberger’s bill helped to clear the way for recent legislation (the Honoring Our PACT Act) that covers all veterans exposed to any biological and environmental hazards, both in the theater of operation and on military bases around the world.”

BACKGROUND

In Congress, Spanberger — the daughter of a U.S. Army Veteran — brought Republicans and Democrats together to expand healthcare coverage and improve job opportunities for Virginia’s Veterans.

Spanberger voted to pass the bipartisan Honoring Our PACT Act, which expanded U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) healthcare and disability benefits for thousands of Virginians who were exposed to burn pits and other toxic substances during the Vietnam War, Gulf War, and post-9/11 eras. The bipartisan law included legislation Abigail led — named after a Veteran from Louisa County — to investigate the health impacts of jet fuel exposure on servicemembers.

The President  also signed into law Spanberger’s bipartisan bill — the Computers for Veterans and Students Act — to provide recycled federal computers to Veterans as they look for new employment opportunities. Last week, Spanberger toured a training facility operated by “Tech for Troops,” a Veteran-operated organization that connects Virginia Veterans with new career opportunities and worked closely with Spanberger to push her legislation over the finish line.

As the next Governor of Virginia, Abigail will work to expand opportunity and make sure our Veterans receive the best possible care — and stand up to attempts to slash support for America’s heroes. Last month, Virginia Veterans launched “Veterans for Spanberger” during an event in Norfolk to demonstrate her growing support among Virginia’s Veterans, servicemembers, and military families.
