By Michael Martz

Gov.-elect Abigail Spanberger‘s choice for agriculture and forestry secretary, Chesterfield County resident Katie Frazier, brings bipartisan appeal in the Democratic-controlled General Assembly.

Frazier, a Shenandoah Valley native, has been chief branding officer at Farm Credit of the Virginias since 2018, and previously served as executive director of the Virginia Agribusiness Council.

Sen. Dave Marsden, D-Fairfax, chair of the Senate Agriculture, Conservation and Natural Resources Committee, said he also has worked closely with Frazier and gave her appointment “a thumbs up.”

“Certainly, she understands the agricultural world,” Marsden said. “She’s been knee-deep in it for many, many years.”

Republicans like her, too.

“I just think very highly of her,” said Del. Lee Ware, R-Powhatan, a member of the House Agriculture, Chesapeake and Natural Resources Committee, who has served on the panel and its predecessors for 28 years. “She is a very steady, knowledgeable and well-regarded choice. I’m delighted.”

Frazier worked closely with Spanberger during her three terms in Congress, when the future governor proudly featured her distinction as “the only Virginian” on the U.S. House Agriculture Committee. She is a member of the governor-elect’s transition team on agriculture and forestry.

“Agriculture is the number-one private industry in Virginia,” Spanberger said in announcing the appointment on Wednesday. “Our commonwealth deserves dedicated leadership that brings Virginia crop and livestock producers, foresters, business leaders, and community members together to protect and grow this critical sector of our economy.”

“That’s why I’m excited to announce the appointment of Katie Frazier to serve as our next Secretary of Agriculture and Forestry,” she said. “Especially at this moment marked by uncertainty across the farm economy, I know that Katie’s decades of supporting Virginia’s growers and their families will provide the steady experience our agriculture and forestry industries need.”

Frazier, a native of Bridgewater in Rockingham County, said she would bring her 20 years of experience “advocating for agriculture, forestry and rural communities.”

“Governor-elect Spanberger has been a steadfast champion and supporter of agriculture and forestry, a commitment that began during her time in Congress serving on the House Agriculture Committee,” she said. “I look forward to collaborating with the Spanberger Administration, the General Assembly, and our partners to continue advancing a vision of economic growth and prosperity for our farmers, foresters, and agribusinesses.”

Frazier will succeed another Rockingham native, Matt Lohr, who serves as agriculture secretary under Gov. Glenn Youngkin, a Republican.

Lohr told Rocktown Now, a Harrisonburg media outlet, on Wednesday that Frazier “has been just a longtime advocate for agriculture.”

“I’ve known Katie since she was in high school and I couldn’t be more … pleased with the selection,” he said.

Frazier’s father, Bill Kyger, is a longtime government teacher at Turner Ashby High School in Bridgewater and a former member of the Rockingham Board of Supervisors for 36 years.

“She’s well-versed in agriculture, having grown up in Rockingham County,” said former Sen. Emmett Hanger, R-Augusta. “I’m very confident that she understands the industry and will be great to work with.”

“She’s not political,” Hanger said. “She’s middle of the road.”
