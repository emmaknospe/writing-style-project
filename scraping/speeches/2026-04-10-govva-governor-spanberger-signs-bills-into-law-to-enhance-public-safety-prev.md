RICHMOND, VA — Governor Abigail Spanberger signed legislation into law to support law enforcement and first responders, keep Virginians safe online and in their communities, and protect kids, communities, and law enforcement officers from gun violence.

“As a former federal law enforcement officer and someone who comes from a law enforcement family, public safety is personal to me,” said Governor Abigail Spanberger. “We are taking concrete steps to make sure Virginia’s law enforcement has the support they need to keep our communities safe.”

The Governor signed bipartisan bills to help local law enforcement officers better coordinate in response to crisis calls, strengthen cyberstalking laws, and protect Virginians from sexual and domestic violence. Additionally, the Governor signed legislation to crack down on “ghost guns” — untraceable firearms without serial numbers that law enforcement cannot track. The Governor also signed a bipartisan bill to close the “intimate partner loophole,” a law that will prohibit convicted domestic abusers from possessing a firearm.

Governor Spanberger added, “Preventing gun violence is an issue of public safety — both for the officers who protect our streets and the children and families they work to keep safe. Whether you’re a first responder or a survivor seeking justice, these laws reflect a simple commitment: the Commonwealth of Virginia will always have your back.”

BACKGROUND

These actions follow last week’s announcement that the Governor signed bipartisan school safety legislation measures to ensure Virginia teachers have the resources and training to respond to emergencies. These include strengthening training on red flag laws, helping teachers identify student mental health challenges, and modernizing internet safety education.

Governor Spanberger has also signed dozens of bipartisan bills to make healthcare, housing, and energy more affordable, accessible, and reliable for Virginians. The Governor signed bills to crack down on rising prescription drug costs, increase the supply of available housing, and protect Virginia families and small businesses from increased energy costs.
