By Andrew Cain

The Virginia Police Benevolent Association, which represents about 11,000 law enforcement officers in Virginia and backed Republican Glenn Youngkin in 2021, announced Wednesday that it is endorsing Democrat Abigail Spanberger for governor.

Joe Woloszyn, the association’s president, said in a statement: “Abigail has a deep, expert-level knowledge of the unique challenges facing Virginia’s law enforcement officers, and she is a leader who shares our goal of building trust across our communities and working towards a safer, stronger Commonwealth.”

The organization is splitting its endorsements, backing Republicans John Reid for lieutenant governor and Attorney General Jason Miyares, who is seeking reelection.

In a message to the division’s members, Woloszyn said: “These endorsements reflect our organization’s commitment to supporting candidates who understand the challenges faced by law enforcement and who will advocate for policies that promote officer benefits, due process, and the well-being of our members.”

Spanberger, who faces Lt. Gov. Winsome Earle-Sears, the GOP nominee, is the first Virginia Democratic candidate for governor since 2009 to receive the group’s endorsement.

Woloszyn noted that Spanberger “grew up in a law enforcement family and is a former federal law enforcement officer herself, so she understands the responsibility of putting on the badge.”

Spanberger’s father was a police officer and she worked as a postal inspector before joining the CIA.

Spanberger said she is honored to receive the group’s endorsement.

“Virginians deserve a Governor who recognizes the tremendous sacrifice that comes with swearing an oath to protect and serve our neighbors. As Governor, I will always make sure our brave men and women in blue have the tools, resources, and training they need to do their jobs and keep us safe.”

The national Fraternal Order of Police recently honored Spanberger and former Rep. Garret Graves, R-La., for their work to pass the Social Security Fairness Act, bipartisan legislation to ensure that police officers and other former public servants receive their full benefits under Social Security.

Reid, who faces state Sen. Ghazala Hashmi, D-Chesterfield, said in a statement: “I’m honored to have the support of the men and women who protect our communities every single day.

“I will always stand with our first responders not just in words, but in action. That means making sure they have the resources, training, and political backing they need to do their jobs and get home safe. In Richmond, I’ll fight to make Virginia the safest state in the country.”

Miyares, seeking a second term, is calling himself the “people’s protector.” He faces former Del. Jay Jones, D-Norfolk.

Miyares said in a statement: “It’s an honor to have the Virginia Police Benevolent Association’s trust and support in this election. Law-and-order leadership matters, and I’m proud to stand with the courageous men and women who wear the badge and protect Virginians every day.”
