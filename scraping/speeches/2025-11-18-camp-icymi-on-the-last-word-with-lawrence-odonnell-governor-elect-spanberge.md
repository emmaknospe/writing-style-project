Governor-Elect Spanberger: “People Want a Governor Who Is Going to Be Responsive to the Challenges That They Are Facing on the Ground and in Their Day-to-Day Lives”

RICHMOND, Va. — Governor-elect Abigail Spanberger continues to make clear that her Administration will be laser-focused on lowering costs for Virginians.

Last night, Spanberger joined MS NOW’s “The Last Word with Lawrence O’Donnell” to highlight the economic concerns she’s hearing from Virginians across the Commonwealth and discuss the message and priorities that led to her victory on November 4.

“People are struggling,” said Spanberger. “People are really having a hard time making ends meet, whether it’s costs at the grocery store, the pharmacy counter, when they get their energy bill, when they sign their kids up for daycare — if they can find a spot; and housing prices continue to go up — whether you’re a renter or buyer. And the reality is that life just continues to be difficult.”

“We saw that in the results of the Virginia election. People want a Governor who is going to be responsive to the challenges that they are facing on the ground and in their day to day lives,” Spanberger continued. “Whether it’s issues of lowering costs, issues of strengthening our schools, or overall safety and security within our communities, people were really responsive to the campaigning that I did in every corner of our Commonwealth focused on really straightforward policies that are going to make an impact on the things that are front of mind for them.”

Spanberger also made clear that her Administration will serve all Virginians, regardless of whether they voted for her or not.

“I think what was important for me was to demonstrate interest and a true desire to get to know people […] in every part of Virginia,” said Spanberger. “Whether they’re historically Republican voting or Democratic voting or they’re toss-up communities, whether they’re down the street from my home or they’re hours and hours away — people want to feel rooted, and people want to feel that their concerns matter and that their voices matter, that their communities matter.”
