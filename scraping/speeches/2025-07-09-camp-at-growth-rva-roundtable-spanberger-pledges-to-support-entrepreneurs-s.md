RICHMOND, Va. — Congresswoman Abigail Spanberger today joined local business leaders in Richmond to discuss the challenges facing Virginia’s small businesses — and made clear that as the next Governor of Virginia, she will prioritize protecting Virginians’ jobs, growing the Commonwealth’s economy, and bringing new business investment to Virginia.

In a conversation with Growth RVA members, Spanberger outlined the concerns she continues to hear from small business owners, contractors, and business leaders across Virginia who are worried about threats to Virginia’s economy. Additionally, she discussed her forward-looking vision to support small businesses, startups, and entrepreneurs as the next Governor of Virginia — as well as her commitment to lowering costs for all Virginians.

“Issues of affordability are pervasive challenges across our communities — cost of living, housing costs, housing supply — and yes, it impacts individuals, but it also impacts business owners. It also impacts people who might be looking to recruit a workforce, or start a business, or grow a business and expand to a particular region where we have a lack of attainable, affordable housing. It becomes such a hurdle for so many, for so many families and for so many business owners,” said Spanberger.

BACKGROUND

Spanberger has long prioritized supporting Virginia businesses and lowering costs for Virginia families.

Earlier this year, Spanberger announced her plan to lower costs for Virginians — her Affordable Virginia Plan, which would lower healthcare, housing, and energy costs for Virginia families.

As a Member of Congress, Spanberger was consistently recognized for her leadership in supporting Virginia businesses and workers and supporting policies that advanced economic growth, helped create jobs, and cut red tape.

In 2020 and 2022, Spanberger was endorsed by the U.S. Chamber of Commerce for her support and advocacy for Virginia’s business community in the U.S. House — including for her role in moving the bipartisan infrastructure law over the finish line. And in June 2020, she received the U.S. Chamber of Commerce’s first-ever “Jefferson-Hamilton Award for Bipartisanship” for her commitment to finding common ground on issues facing Virginia’s small businesses community and overall economy.
