Governor-Elect Spanberger: “You Can’t Just Be About ‘Vote Against This Person.’ We’re Trying to Deliver an Economic Message on Affordability”

RICHMOND, Va. — Governor-elect Abigail Spanberger yesterday joined “The Weekly Show with Jon Stewart” — together with New Jersey Governor-elect Mikie Sherrill — to discuss how she delivered results for Virginians as a Member of Congress and how she’ll lead as the next Governor of Virginia.

During the interview, Spanberger highlighted her bipartisan efforts to pass the Social Security Fairness Act. The Spanberger-led legislation, which the President signed into law earlier this year, has already delivered more than $300 million in earned retirements to more than 46,000 Virginia public servants across the Commonwealth.

“I represented a district that was very heavily public servants, retired police officers, retired federal employees — and there was this little provision in social security law that basically stole people’s social security benefits,” said Spanberger. “People who were headed off into their retirement get their first social security check and it’s fractions of what they thought they would get. This law went into place back in the ’80s. I get to Congress saying, ‘Oh my goodness, okay, we’re going to fix this.’”

“And where it was really horrible is if someone lost their spouse. They themselves had worked throughout their career. They lost a spouse who had been a firefighter, police officer, they saw their own, as the surviving spouse, their social security benefits cut. I mean, people were thrust into poverty. People lost their homes. It’s an outrage,” Spanberger continued. “We’d have all these co-sponsors, but then year after year, we couldn’t actually get leadership, Democratic or Republican leadership, to bring it for a vote.”

Spanberger concluded, “We ended up doing a discharge petition because we couldn’t get leadership to bring it up. We forced the hands of leadership in the House. So we got it through. We got it signed into law. It was the last bill signing that President Biden did before he left. And it’s changed lives everywhere across Virginia, and I got letters from across the country.”

Spanberger also spoke about how she centered her campaign around the top-of-mind concerns she heard from Virginians — including the Trump Administration’s attacks on Virginia’s economy.

“You can’t just be about ‘vote against this person.’ We’re trying to deliver an economic message on affordability,” said Spanberger. “The fact that you have an attack on our entire economy, and our Governor, our current Republican Governor and Lieutenant Governor have said nothing, I mean, it’s an abdication of responsibility.”

Spanberger then discussed her work to increase transparency and trust in Congress, including her bipartisan legislation to ban Members of Congress, their spouses, and children from trading stocks.

“Whether you think that people are intentionally trying to get rich off of information or not kind of doesn’t matter. The fact that everybody thinks and it’s being reported as though it’s fact — that you’re in this meeting and then you buy this stock therefore it’s corruption,” said Spanberger. “We said, ‘This is wrong. Let’s write a bill to fix it.’”

“You’re being responsive to the things that you’re hearing on the ground and what the American people or your constituents want,” Spanberger continued. “People deserve to trust their government and we should take proactive actions, right, to help them do that.”
