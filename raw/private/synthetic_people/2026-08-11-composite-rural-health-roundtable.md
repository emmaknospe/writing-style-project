**WHY THIS DOCUMENT**

We have committed publicly to "a series of roundtables and listening sessions" on the
federal healthcare and food cuts, and we've held convenings with health plans,
hospitals, providers, free and charitable clinics, community organizations, and
philanthropies. The next one should be in Southwest.

Before we build the invite list, this memo sketches **the four kinds of participant we
expect in the room** as composites, so we can decide what we're prepared to say to each
of them. Substitute real names once scheduling confirms.

---

## COMPOSITE A — "SHERRY LANDIS," CEO, 25-bed critical access hospital

Runs the only inpatient facility in a two-county area. Operating margin was thin before
H.R. 1 and her board is now modeling 2028.

**Her first question will be about the cliff.** She knows the Virginia number —
**$1.8 to $2.6 billion per year at minimum** in lost federal funds **beginning in
2028** — and she knows her budget cycle doesn't let her wait until 2027 to react. She
will ask whether the **$350M Medicaid Reserve Fund** is available to institutions like
hers or only to coverage.

**We need an answer to that before the room, not in it.** If the honest answer is that
the Reserve Fund is structured around protecting coverage rather than backstopping
facility operations, say that plainly and pivot to Rural Health Transformation. Sherry
will forgive a limit. She will not forgive being managed.

**What we can offer credibly:** nearly **$1B** in the biennium to contend with the
worst impacts; **$189.5M** in year-one Rural Health Transformation funding; the
Governor's stated commitment to bring providers, patients, and local leaders to the
table as the path forward is charted.

## COMPOSITE B — "DR. AMARA NKEMDIRIM," family physician, two-clinic practice

Sees Medicaid, Marketplace, and uninsured patients in the same afternoon. Her concern
is not the top-line cut; it's **churn** — eligible patients losing coverage over
paperwork.

This is our strongest ground. **Removing administrative barriers to keep people
covered** is a live workstream, and Sec. Figueroa's message discipline was built for
her: *"if you are eligible for health coverage, we want to help you keep it."* The
**$150M premium assistance program** for Marketplace enrollees is directly relevant to
her panel.

She will also raise the **Medicare Part D subsidy program** ending — **nearly 690,000
Virginia seniors** statewide use it — because her older patients are already asking.
We have described the problem publicly; we have not announced a state response to that
specific change. Don't invent one.

## COMPOSITE C — "WENDELL PRATT," county administrator

Not a health professional. He's here because the hospital is his county's second-largest
employer and he is doing arithmetic about the tax base.

For him the healthcare story is an **economic development** story, and we should let it
be one. The framing already exists in our own material — **Homegrown Health Heroes**,
built on partnerships with public education, community colleges, and local providers,
and the first **Rural Health Transformation** award: **$127,500** to **Virginia
Highlands Community College** in Abingdon to expand its **LPN program** with more
course offerings, more student capacity, and instructional support.

That grant is the single most useful thing we can put in front of Wendell, because it
is small, specific, already out the door, and in his region.

## COMPOSITE D — "BRENDA COYLE," volunteer EMS captain and part-time home care aide

The person the system runs on and the one least likely to speak first. Two things in
our record are hers:

- the budget amendment providing **lifesaving cancer screenings for Virginia
  firefighters**
- the budget amendment providing **earlier salary increases for home care workers**

Both were among the fourteen amendments the General Assembly accepted. Neither is
well known outside Richmond. Ask her directly whether anyone has told her about them —
if the answer is no, that's a finding worth carrying back.

---

**RECOMMENDED SHAPE**

Ninety minutes. Providers speak first and longest. The Governor's opening should be
under four minutes and should include the limit she always states — *we cannot undo all
the harm Washington is inflicting* — because in a room like this, overpromising is the
failure mode, not underpromising.

Close with the referral, as always: **DMAS** for Medicaid questions, **DSS** for SNAP.

**Open item:** decide before we schedule whether visiting facilities from the Joint
Commission on Healthcare's at-risk list is systematic or opportunistic. Tappahannock
was on that list and the visit worked. If there's a sequence, Southwest should be next
and we should say so internally.
