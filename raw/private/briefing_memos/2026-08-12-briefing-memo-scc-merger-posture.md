**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Governor Spanberger
**FROM:** Policy / Energy, with Communications
**DATE:** August 12, 2026
**RE:** NextEra–Dominion intervention — posture and likely questions

---

## 1. WHERE WE ARE

Six days out from the WaPo op-ed. Two national/regional hits behind us (FOX 5 DC 8/9,
CNBC 8/11). The intervention is now the frame through which most energy coverage will
run for the foreseeable term of the SCC's review.

The sequencing has been good for us and is worth protecting: the **SCC transmission
order landed August 5**, one day before the op-ed. The op-ed used it as evidence, not
as a coincidence — "this week — after she urged state regulators to protect Virginians
from shouldering new energy costs, the SCC ordered data centers to cover the cost of
transmission infrastructure built exclusively for those facilities." That's the
strongest available answer to "does intervening actually accomplish anything." We have
a receipt. Lead with it.

## 2. THE POSTURE, STATED PRECISELY

Three things must stay true in every answer:

1. **Skeptical, not opposed.** "Deeply skeptical about whether selling our primary,
   state-regulated utility to an out-of-state company is good for the commonwealth."
   Skepticism plus questions. We have not announced a position on approval.
2. **A voice, not a verdict.** "Taking this action does not mean I intend to make the
   SCC's decision for it." The commissioners decide. We are a party, seeking answers,
   information, and conditions that benefit Virginians.
3. **Three non-negotiables, always three, always in order.** Bills → workforce →
   energy future. The repetition across the op-ed, FOX 5, and CNBC is an asset; the
   first time the list changes shape it becomes a story.

## 3. HISTORY WORTH HAVING IN MIND

- **First time a Governor of Virginia has intervened at the SCC.** Expect this framed
  as overreach. Her own answer preempts it: unprecedented action, but "so, too, is the
  size of this proposed merger and its potential impact on the commonwealth."
- Agencies under the Governor — DEQ among them — participate in SCC cases routinely.
  Direct gubernatorial involvement is what's rare. Useful distinction if the "novel
  intrusion" line comes at us.
- The July 9 filing through the Chief Energy Officer was itself described in coverage
  as a rare move. We have now done the rare thing twice and won the first one.
- The SCC's own mandate — "balance the interests of citizens, businesses, and
  customers" — is in the statute, not our framing. Use it.

## 4. LIKELY QUESTIONS

**Q: Are you trying to block this merger?**
No. Intervening makes us a party so Virginians have a voice; the commissioners decide.
We have three non-negotiables and we will judge the deal against them.

**Q: Isn't this political interference in an independent regulator?**
The SCC decides. We are asking to participate in a proceeding whose outcome will shape
energy affordability in Virginia for decades. Participation is what intervention *is*.

**Q: What specifically would make you support it?**
Long-term, sustained savings on the monthly bill. Binding assurance on Virginia utility
jobs. A clear plan to accelerate — not slow or trade away — affordable, reliable,
local, clean power. Resist naming a dollar figure or a jobs number; we have not put one
on the record and should not do it extemporaneously.

**Q: NextEra will say the deal delivers scale and savings.**
Then they should be able to demonstrate it to the SCC, on the record, with conditions
we can hold them to. That is exactly why we want to be at the table.

**Q: Why should Virginians trust that this does anything?**
August 5. The SCC ordered data centers to pay for transmission built exclusively for
them, projected to save Virginians hundreds of millions of dollars, after our filing.

**Q: Are you skeptical of out-of-state ownership generally?**
Stay on this deal. The concern is specific: our primary, state-regulated utility, the
largest such merger contemplated here, and what it means for bills, workers, and our
energy future.

**Q: Data centers — moratorium?**
We have no position on the record. Do not make one here. Pivot to the consumption tax,
the transmission order, the generator and water standards, and local authority.

## 5. WHAT WE STILL OWE

- Procedural schedule from the SCC once our petition is acted on. Comms wants notice
  before it posts.
- A workforce-facing plan. The op-ed named linemen, grid operators, and engineers
  specifically and we have not yet done anything visible with them. See the composite
  stakeholder memo in synthetic_people/ for the outreach shape we discussed.
- Coordination with the legislators who signed the VanValkenburg letter on
  transmission. They moved with us once; the merger is a bigger ask.
