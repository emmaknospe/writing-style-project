**SITUATION**

Tomorrow is the **61st anniversary** of Medicare and Medicaid being signed into law. We
are marking it against the largest cuts to Medicaid in the program's history.

The anniversary is a good vehicle for two reasons. It lets us talk about the programs
as things people *use* rather than line items, and it gives us a natural place to
publish what the new dashboards are showing.

**THE NUMBERS WE'RE PUTTING OUT**

- **300,000 Virginians** currently covered through Medicaid have coverage threatened.
  This is dashboard-derived. Attribute it that way.
- **$1 trillion** in Medicaid cuts over ten years nationwide.
- **$1.8–2.6 billion per year at minimum** in lost federal funds in Virginia alone,
  **beginning in 2028**. The start date matters — this is not yet visible in most
  people's lives, which is both our warning and our window.
- **$187 billion** cut from federal SNAP over the decade.
- H.R. 1 also **weakens Medicare's authority to negotiate prescription drug prices** —
  which you voted to establish in Congress. Worth saying in the first person.
- **New this week:** the administration in Washington announced it will end a
  **Medicare Part D subsidy program** used by **nearly 690,000 Virginia seniors** to
  hold down prescription premiums. Tens of millions of seniors nationally.

*Number discipline.* We used **more than 550,000** at the July 7 roundtable — that was
healthcare access threatened. Tomorrow's 300,000 is Medicaid coverage at risk per the
dashboards. Both are ours; they measure different things. If a reporter puts them side
by side, explain the difference rather than choosing one retroactively.

**WHAT WE HAVE ALREADY DONE**

Budget, 2026–2028 biennium — **nearly $1 billion** to contend with the worst impacts:

- **$350M** Medicaid Reserve Fund — prepare for the federal cuts, protect access
- **$150M** targeted Marketplace premium assistance for Virginians losing affordable
  coverage

Operationally:

- Sec. Figueroa and agencies across state government directed to assess system impacts
  and connect Virginians with additional coverage options
- **Removing administrative barriers** so eligible people don't lose coverage for
  paperwork reasons — this is the least glamorous and most consequential thing we're
  doing
- **Dashboards** to monitor impacts as they land
- **Roundtables and listening sessions** — patients, providers, health plans,
  hospitals, community-based organizations, philanthropies. Two held to date; the July
  7 convening drew the Virginia Association of Free and Charitable Clinics, whose CEO
  Rufus Phillips made the paperwork-burden point for us better than we could
- **Rural Health Transformation** — $189.5M year one, first award already out the door
  to Virginia Highlands CC in Abingdon
- **Day One EO** stood up the Economic Resiliency Task Force, which directs agencies to
  implement H.R. 1's Medicaid and SNAP changes *while protecting eligible access*

**RECOMMENDED MESSAGE**

Lead with the people, not the mechanism. The anniversary framing you've used before
works: these programs are how millions of Virginians "see a doctor, fill a
prescription, and care for a loved one."

Then the limit, stated honestly — *we cannot undo all the harm Washington is
inflicting* — followed immediately by the tools. The honesty is what makes the tools
believable. Don't drop it for a cleaner soundbite.

Close on the direct instruction: if you're worried about your coverage, DMAS for
Medicaid, DSS for SNAP. Every hit on this should end with somewhere to go.

**OPEN ITEMS**

1. Roundtable cadence — we've said "a series." Three is a series; one more is not.
   Recommend locking two more before Labor Day, at least one in Southwest.
2. The 2028 cliff needs a public-facing explainer. Right now the biggest number in this
   file is also the one furthest away, and that is a communications problem.
3. Rural hospital list — the Joint Commission's at-risk report is doing a lot of work
   for us. We should decide whether we're visiting more of those facilities
   systematically or opportunistically.
