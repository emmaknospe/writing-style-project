# FACT SHEET — DATA CENTERS

Rev. 3 · August 13, 2026 · Policy desk
Supersedes the July 24 version (pre-dates the SCC order).

### THE FRAME

"Data centers pay their fair share." Three levers, all now in motion: **a consumption
tax**, **transmission cost allocation**, and **environmental + local-authority
standards**. She has never framed this as growth vs. no-growth — the framing is who
pays and to what standard.

---

### 1. STATEWIDE ENERGY CONSUMPTION TAX

- **First-of-its-kind** statewide tax on data centers' energy consumption.
- Proposed by the Governor **spring 2026**; enacted in the **2026–2028 biennium
  budget**, finalized June 29.
- Her framing (POLITICO Energy, week of 7/6): "We want data centers to pay their fair
  share — the way to get at that is by ensuring that they're paying this consumption
  tax, based on their energy consumption."
- She pairs it forward-looking with "longer-term views of what are the standards we
  want to set in Virginia for water usage, for backup energy generation."
- Repeatedly flagged as a floor, not a ceiling: *"But this is only the beginning."*

### 2. TRANSMISSION COSTS — THE SCC ORDER

**The win.** On **August 5, 2026** the SCC ordered data centers to cover the cost of
transmission infrastructure **built exclusively for those facilities**. Projected to
save Virginia ratepayers **hundreds of millions of dollars**.

How it happened:

- **July 9** — Chief Energy Officer **Josephus Allmond**, at the Governor's direction,
  filed comments with the SCC on behalf of Virginia ratepayers. Rare action by a
  sitting Virginia governor; agencies (e.g. DEQ) file routinely, governors do not.
- Filing language: "It is a core policy of the Administration... to foster an
  affordable, reliable and clean energy system... This includes strong support for
  proactive, right sized, and cost-effective transmission development."
- **Deputy Chief Energy Officer Louise White** testified: "Any network or substation
  upgrades that would not have been triggered but for a large load customer should be
  assigned directly to that customer, shielding regular Virginia families from
  subsidizing commercial extension."
- The administration endorsed the **"but-for" test** and **mandatory upfront
  payments** — a *contribution in aid of construction*, so the expense never enters
  the pool of costs spread across everyone's bills.
- Also urging the but-for test: **PEC**, **Old Dominion Electric Cooperative**, and
  **the SCC's own staff**.
- **Sen. Schuyler VanValkenburg + 19 other state legislators** wrote the SCC endorsing
  the effort. VanValkenburg: "This protects Virginians from higher utility bills and
  assigns the cost where it belongs."

**The underlying case.** Dominion sought recovery of roughly **$1.5 billion** in
high-voltage transmission investments. Under the prior system those costs were
allocated across *all* Dominion customer classes — residential and small business
included.

Allmond on the order: "Instead of spreading transmission costs caused directly by
data centers across all ratepayers, now data centers will pay for those costs
directly... a vanguard decision."

### 3. STANDARDS & LOCAL AUTHORITY

Signed **June 30, 2026** (package incl. Affordable Virginia Agenda bills):

- stricter emissions standards on data center **backup / diesel generators**
- parameters on **noise pollution** and **water-related technology**
- **new tools for localities** to assess the impact of data center projects on homes,
  schools, and farmland
- protection for ratepayers from shouldering the cost of new energy infrastructure
  investments by data centers and other high-load customers

### 4. REGIONAL — PJM

She joined a **bipartisan group of governors** pressing **PJM Interconnection**
(operator of the nation's largest grid) to make sure data centers pay for new
**capacity** costs. The Chief Energy Officer works "directly with PJM" on changes to
drive additional generation and get newer generation into the PJM marketplace —
"ultimately to the benefit of ratepayers across Virginia."
