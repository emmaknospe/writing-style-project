## THE DEAL

- NextEra Energy — Florida-based — has filed paperwork to buy **Dominion Energy for
  about $67 billion**.
- Would create **the largest regulated electric utility in the world**.
- Reviewing body: **Virginia State Corporation Commission (SCC)**. Commissioners can
  **approve, deny, or impose new conditions**.
- Governor's characterization: "wholly unprecedented in Virginia's history."

## THE ACTION

The Governor is **formally intervening** — legally requesting to be a party to the
case. **First time a Governor of Virginia has intervened at the SCC.**

Announced Thursday, **August 6, 2026**, in a Washington Post op-ed ("Why I'm
intervening in the Dominion-NextEra merger").

What intervening gets the administration:

- legal right to engage in the proceeding
- standing to bring concerns forward
- ability to request detailed information about the proposed merger
- a venue to make clear Virginians expect long-term, tangible benefits

**What it is not.** Her own framing, verbatim and worth holding to: "Taking this
action does not mean I intend to make the SCC's decision for it. Instead, I am
seeking to make sure Virginians have a voice in the process."

## THE THREE NON-NEGOTIABLES

These are stated the same way in the op-ed, on FOX 5 DC, and on CNBC. Do not
paraphrase loosely — the consistency is the point.

**1. Virginia's families and small businesses.**
"If two large corporations stand to benefit financially from this merger, so, too,
should the Virginians who pay the bills." Any deal must deliver a more affordable
energy bill with **sustained, long-term** cost savings.

**2. Virginia's workforce.**
Named explicitly: *the linemen who get the lights back on after a rough storm, the
grid operators who monitor real-time power generation, the engineers who make sure
projects follow environmental laws.* Steady, good-paying jobs must not go anywhere
even if an out-of-state company acquires their employer.

**3. Virginia's energy future.**
Any owner of the state's largest regulated utility must have a **clear plan to
accelerate** progress toward affordable, reliable, local and clean power — "not slow
it down or trade it away."

## POSTURE

She is **deeply skeptical** — her word — "about whether selling our primary,
state-regulated utility to an out-of-state company is good for the commonwealth."
Skeptical, with questions, seeking answers. Not opposed on the record.

The SCC's mandate requires commissioners to "balance the interests of citizens,
businesses, and customers." Her line: "for a deal this consequential, I will not
watch from the sidelines as that potential balance is assessed and determined."
