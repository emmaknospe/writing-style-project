## THE ARC

She has described her early read on the system bluntly, and the phrasing is
deliberate — the problem is the system, not the workforce:

> "When I came into office, I got an early window into the many things that were wrong
> inside Virginia's correctional system. Not wrong because of the dedicated VADOC
> employees who work tirelessly day in and day out — but systemically wrong."

The reform program was announced **June 23, 2026** at VADOC headquarters in Richmond,
alongside corrections officers, public safety leaders, and advocates. The stated aims:
safety for **officers, staff, and incarcerated individuals**; transparency; and
accountability. A public report was released the same day.

## THE COUNCIL

**Governor's Community Partnership Council on Corrections** — announced June 23,
applications opened **August 4, 2026**.

- **Advisory** council; a permanent, structured forum for dialogue and action
- **Led by** Sec. of Public Safety and Homeland Security **Stanley Meador** and VADOC
  Director **Joseph Walters**
- **No more than 18 members**
- Application deadline: **Friday, September 4, 2026**

**Five subject areas**, always listed in this order:
1. conditions of confinement
2. reentry and reintegration
3. family engagement
4. staffing
5. public accountability

**Who it hears from:** corrections officers and VADOC staff; incarcerated and formerly
incarcerated individuals; families; communities across Virginia.

The durability argument is hers and is the most quotable thing in the file: forming
this group "is exactly how we ensure that these reforms we've undertaken take root, and
build a foundation for Virginia long after I am no longer in this office."

Meador's companion framing: "Engagement isn't an event; it's a practice." Walters
describes a "people-first culture" and a "new approach over these last five months."

## REENTRY

Virginia's **Intensive Reentry Programs** run on a **Cognitive Community model** built
on accountability, support, and structure — emphasizing **cognitive restructuring**, an
evidence-based approach to changing thinking patterns, building emotional maturity, and
strengthening decision-making. Programming covers reentry preparation, workforce
readiness, and connections to community resources.

**August 5, 2026 — Red Onion State Prison.** The IRP launched there is **among the
first of its kind in the nation at a maximum-security facility**, and is the **15th** in
operation across VADOC facilities at various security levels. Preparation included
corrections team members visiting **Coffeewood Correctional Center**, where an IRP has
run successfully for more than a decade. Initial space is limited; eligibility criteria
apply.

Her line, which is the whole argument in one sentence: **"Reentry programming should
not stop at the doors of our maximum-security facilities."** The justification offered
is public safety, not mercy — "long-term public safety requires preparing individuals
for a successful, lawful return to their communities, regardless of where they serve
their sentence."
