## 1. WHY ENERGY IS AN AFFORDABILITY FILE

Energy is not filed here as an environmental issue. It is filed as a **cost-of-living**
issue, and that is deliberate. The through-line from the campaign to the current term
is the cost of living; energy is where that cost has moved fastest and where the
Commonwealth has the most direct levers.

Her own accounting of the pressures, from the POLITICO Energy interview (July 2026):
rising rates and bills; "real chaos coming out of Washington with trade and tariff
policies being disruptive"; **H.R. 1 driving up energy costs**; and the war in Iran,
since the beginning of which estimates put an **additional $1,000** in expenses on the
average family.

Everything below is organized around one question: who pays, and can we make the answer
fairer without slowing generation.

## 2. THE INSTITUTIONAL PIECE — CHIEF ENERGY OFFICER

She **created** the role of **Chief Energy Officer** as a **cabinet-level** position
and appointed **Josephus Allmond** (announced March 2026). **Louise White** serves as
Deputy Chief Energy Officer.

The role is deliberately cross-cutting. In her description, it "works across our
secretariats to find places where there are additional levers that we can pull on to
lower costs for people."

In practice the office has been the administration's instrument in three places:

- **the SCC** — the July 9 transmission filing, and the testimony behind it
- **PJM** — direct engagement on market changes
- **program delivery** — Switch Together, VE Connect, the Energy Plan process

This matters procedurally: filings go in through the Chief Energy Officer, which is how
the administration participates in regulatory proceedings without the Governor
personally being a party. The NextEra–Dominion intervention is the exception, and the
first of its kind.

## 3. THE SCC

The **State Corporation Commission** regulates utilities in Virginia. Its statutory
mandate requires commissioners to **"balance the interests of citizens, businesses, and
customers."**

Two administration actions before it, both unusual:

**a. Transmission cost allocation (July–August 2026).** Filing July 9 through the Chief
Energy Officer, urging that costs of transmission projects driven by data center needs
be assigned to those facilities. The administration endorsed the **"but-for" test** —
if a line would never have been built but for the data center it serves, that facility
pays the entire cost — and **mandatory upfront payment** via *contribution in aid of
construction*, keeping the expense out of the pool spread across all customers.

Context: Dominion sought recovery of roughly **$1.5B** in high-voltage transmission
investments. Prior practice allocated such costs across *all* customer classes,
residential and small business included. **PEC**, **Old Dominion Electric
Cooperative**, and **SCC staff** urged the same test. Twenty legislators, led by
**Sen. Schuyler VanValkenburg**, wrote in support.

**Outcome: August 5, 2026 — the SCC so ordered.** Projected savings to Virginia
ratepayers: **hundreds of millions of dollars.**

**b. NextEra–Dominion merger (August 2026 →).** Formal intervention; first ever by a
Virginia Governor. See the dedicated fact sheet.

## 4. RGGI

Virginia **rejoined the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative** by legislation signed
**June 30, 2026**. Proceeds fund energy efficiency and flood prevention.

The politically important half is the **RGGI Consumer Credit**, built into the biennium
budget: a **direct credit to residential customers and small businesses**, designed to
offset the monthly charge that comes with rejoining. Her projection: it will offset it
"and then some," with estimates that average monthly bills **could drop by as much as
$36 annually**. A budget amendment she requested extends RGGI energy credits to
customers served by **rural electric cooperatives**.

Supporting arithmetic the administration uses publicly:

- every $1 on energy conservation reduces utility bills by **$2–4**
- every $1 in flood protection saves approximately **$6** in future damage costs
- across the nationwide program, **$5.7B** in auction proceeds invested to date are
  projected to yield **$22.6B** in total consumer bill savings

**Sec. of Natural and Historic Resources David Bulova** carries the resilience half:
flooding is Virginia's most frequent and costly natural hazard.

Allmond's framing of the guardrails: revenue generated from participation is returned
to Virginians; the credit "will insulate our families and small businesses while still
leaving record levels of investment into energy efficiency and resilience funding."

## 5. GENERATION AND DEMAND

The demand side is data centers (own fact sheet). The supply side:

- **Legislation signed June 30** setting goals to **increase energy storage** and lower
  peak prices, and to **reduce heating and energy costs** for families who need it most
- **PJM** — she joined a **bipartisan group of governors** pressing the operator of the
  nation's largest grid to ensure data centers pay for new **capacity** costs; the
  Chief Energy Officer works directly with PJM to drive additional generation and get
  newer generation into the marketplace
- Longstanding position: "What we really need to do is generate more energy here at
  home," including the **offshore wind** installation
- The stated goal is consistently four adjectives in this order: **affordable,
  reliable, local, clean**

## 6. PROGRAMS VIRGINIANS CAN ACTUALLY USE

**Switch Together** (announced July 13, 2026) — Virginia is the **first state in the
nation** to offer this statewide. An **optional solar group purchasing** initiative run
as a **reverse auction** among qualified installers.

- average **23% saving** on home and business solar installations
- equivalent to **$6,300** on a typical system
- estimated **~$2,200/year** household electricity savings (U.S. Treasury figure)
- discounts also available on **battery storage** and, in many locations, **heat pumps**
- **no direct expenditure of state funds**
- partner: **Solar United Neighbors** (Virginia Program Director **Brandon Praileau**)
- 2026 campaign open **through October 15**, available in **100+ localities**, expanding

Fairfax County's Office of Environmental and Energy Coordination (**John Morrill**) is
the model locality — group purchasing there delivered lower bills, lower emissions, and
grid benefit.

**VE Connect** — the Commonwealth's one-stop-shop for programs and incentives to reduce
electricity bills. Every energy answer to a constituent should end here.

## 7. IN PROGRESS

- **2026 Virginia Energy Plan** — public input sought beginning June 11, 2026. This is
  where the longer-term standards conversation lands: her stated intent is to pair the
  consumption tax with "longer-term views of what are the standards we want to set in
  Virginia for water usage, for backup energy generation."
- Continued press for **commonsense environmental standards for data centers**, named
  explicitly in the six-month retrospective as a priority for the months ahead.
- **NextEra–Dominion** proceeding.
