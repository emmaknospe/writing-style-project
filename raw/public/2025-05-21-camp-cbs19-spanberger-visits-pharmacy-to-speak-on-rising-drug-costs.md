By Sarah Allen

MECHANICSVILLE, Va. (CBS19 NEWS) — Democratic candidate for governor Abigail Spanberger stopped by a family-owned pharmacy outside of Richmond to outline her stance on drug costs in the commonwealth. She says as governor, her first priority would be to make Virginia more affordable.

“All Virginians can count on me to defend their access to the coverage and prescription drugs their doctors prescribe,” Spanberger said.

With nearly 2 million Virginians on Medicaid, the former 7th District representative is making affordable healthcare a priority in her campaign. She says her time on Capitol Hill will be a valuable experience if she is elected to the governor’s mansion.

“I voted to lower health care costs, I fought to bring down prescription drug costs, and I stood up to big pharmaceutical companies who keep drug costs high,” Spanberger said.

Wednesday, Spanberger stopped by the Mechanicsville Drug Store, a family-owned pharmacy since 1957, to speak with constituents who have seen the prescription price tags firsthand.

Long-time Virginia resident Aida Pacheco says her daughter’s breast cancer diagnosis left her family shattered, and potential cuts to Medicaid only made it worse.

“For people like my daughter, this would mean one of the most important tools to fight her battle against cancer. It would mean forcing 630,000 Virginians to lose their care and be one hospital visit away from bankruptcy,” Pacheco said.

And if those cuts to Medicaid go through Congress, Spanberger says pharmacies like the Mechanicsville Drug Store may not be able to keep their doors open, because pharmacy benefit managers, which is essentially a middleman between drug companies, pharmacies and providers, hold all the power.

“As large PBMs have squeezed out some of the local, family-owned and particularly rural pharmacies, I want to make sure Virginians can go to the pharmacy of their choice,” Spanberger said.

Spanberger outlined plans if she were to be elected, including a single Virginia-run PBM for people using Medicaid, as well as expanding telehealth programs for people living in rural areas.
