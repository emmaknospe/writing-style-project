By Mauricio Casillas

It’s a county that’s stood by Republicans for years — but not this time.

Virginia Governor-elect Abigail Spanberger turned Spotsylvania County blue in one of the Virginia election’s biggest surprises.

The county has long been considered a Republican stronghold.

Voters News4 spoke with Wednesday — some in favor of Democrats, others against — shared what they think is driving the change.

“The price of everything,” said voter Larry Moppins. “The things that Mr. Trump is doing in his office, all his immigration stuff, we had to do the Democrats this time.”

“I really think it came down to governmental firings,” said another voter. “[…] I do understand it. There’s a lot of people hurting in a lot of places, but that don’t mean I agree with it.”

One man said he supported President Donald Trump in 2024 but voted for Abigail Spanberger this year because he’s frustrated with the way things are going.

“Happy to see Abigail in there. The rest of them, I think I would’ve preferred other candidates,” he said. “[…] She seems to be pretty level-headed. We’ll see what she does now though.”

And it wasn’t just a win for Democrats at the top of the ticket.

“People were pissed,” said Virginia District 66 Delegate-elect Nicole Cole.

Cole defeated 35-year incumbent Bobby Orrock for the House of Delegates seat that covers parts of Caroline and Spotsylvania counties. She says federal job cuts were a major issue for voters.

“It was not just the federal employees — the contractors, the non-profit organizations that were supported by funds that were taken away by this federal government,” Cole said.

Abigail Spanberger is projected to beat Winsome Earle-Sears by just over 2%. Compare that to the 2024 presidential race, in which Trump won the county by more than eight points.

In 2021, an even wider margin for Governor Glenn Youngkin, who carried Spotsylvania by more than 20.

The closest a Democrat had come before this was in 2018, when Sen. Tim Kaine, who won statewide by 16%, lost Spotsylvania by fewer than four points.

Analysts also point to a changing electorate as a key factor, with more residents from Northern Virginia’s urban counties relocating to Spotsylvania County.

“The people in the last 20 years who have moved here have moved here largely for issues of affordability,” said University of Mary Washington political scientist Stephen Farnsworth. “And those people are really changing the dynamics of this part of Virginia.”
