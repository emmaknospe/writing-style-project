By Michael Martz

With the election for governor three weeks away, Democrat Abigail Spanberger is getting some outside help to get out the vote. Former U.S. Secretary of Transportation Pete Buttigieg will appear with the Democratic gubernatorial candidate at a rally in Charlottesville on Oct. 21.

The rally is scheduled from 6:30 to 8:30 p.m. next Tuesday, but the Spanberger campaign will release the exact location when people register to attend. Previously, Spanberger had advertised that the rally would feature “a special guest.”

Buttigieg, a former Democratic presidential candidate who is mentioned nationally as a potential presidential candidate in 2028, knows Spanberger well from her three terms in Congress, representing the 7th Congressional District.

He appeared with her in Richmond and Henrico County in late 2021 to pitch federal investments in public infrastructure under then-President Joe Biden and tour the city’s Jackson Ward neighborhood, a historically Black community that construction of Interstate 95 divided in the mid-1950s.

On this visit, he’s focused on the Virginia governor’s race and what he terms its importance amid actions by President Donald Trump, who has rolled back many federal investments made under laws that Congress passed under Biden to boost spending on public infrastructure projects and clean energy production.

Buttigieg announced his visit two weeks into a federal government shutdown as congressional Democrats continue to block a short-term government spending resolution until Trump and congressional Republicans agree to negotiate changes.

Most notably, they want the president and congressional Republicans to agree to extend federal health insurance subsidies that otherwise will expire at the end of the year and substantially raise monthly insurance premiums for Virginia households.

Democrats also want to roll back cuts to Medicaid funding under a tax-cut package that Trump signed into law July 4 that they say will cause hundreds of thousands of Virginians to lose their health care coverage and threaten funding for rural hospitals operating on slim margins.

“Pete knows and understands the stakes for the election this November,” a spokesman said in a statement Monday.

“Abigail Spanberger has a plan to lower costs and create jobs for Virginia families, and she will stand up to Trump and his allies who are putting rural hospitals on the brink of closure and cutting health care for thousands of Virginians.”

Buttigieg is no stranger to Virginia politics. He endorsed former Richmond Mayor Levar Stoney in the Democratic primary for lieutenant governor, which state Sen. Ghazala Hashmi, D-Chesterfield, won in June.

Hashmi faces Republican John Reid in the general election in November. Reid is an openly gay, former Richmond radio host whom Gov. Glenn Youngkin unsuccessfully tried to force out of the lieutenant governor’s race this year over racy photos that were reposted from an online account. Reid says he did not repost the photos.

Buttigieg is an openly gay former Indiana mayor who is married to another man, with whom he has two children.

Lt. Gov. Winsome Earle-Sears, the Republican nominee for governor, opposes same-sex marriage on what she calls moral and religious grounds. Earle-Sears maintained her opposition during a televised debate last week, saying that her position on same-sex marriage is “not discrimination.”
