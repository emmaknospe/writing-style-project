By Andrew Cain

Gov.-elect Abigail Spanberger on Sunday urged both parties to end the shutdown and reopen the federal government immediately.

On Sunday night, U.S. senators reportedly were close to a deal to reopen the government. According to multiple reports, the deal would extend funding through Jan. 30, allow a Senate vote on a Democratic measure to extend Obamacare tax credits, reverse reductions in force from the shutdown and award back pay. The deal also would extend federal payments of SNAP benefits.

Virginia’s U.S. senators split on the proposed deal. Sen. Tim Kaine, D-Va., said Sunday night that he would vote to advance the measure. Sen. Mark Warner, D-Va., said he will oppose it because he thinks the mere promise of a vote on extending health care subsidies is insufficient.

Kaine said in a statement: “I have long said that to earn my vote, we need to be on a path toward fixing Republicans’ health care mess and to protect the federal workforce.

“This deal guarantees a vote to extend Affordable Care Act premium tax credits, which Republicans weren’t willing to do. Lawmakers know their constituents expect them to vote for it, and if they don’t, they could very well be replaced at the ballot box by someone who will.”

Warner, who is up for reelection in 2026, said in a statement: “I appreciate that this proposal includes important language preventing further mass layoffs of federal employees,” calling it a critical step.

“But I cannot support a deal that still leaves millions of Americans wondering how they are going to pay for their health care or whether they will be able to afford to get sick,” Warner said.

Earlier Sunday, Spanberger said on the CBS program “Face the Nation”: “Virginians need to and Virginians want to see the government reopen, and my expectation is that we will see a Congress, a Senate and ultimately a president driving us in that direction.”

Moderator Margaret Brennan asked Spanberger whether Congressional Democrats should vote to reopen the government before negotiating about health care issues.

“The government needs to open and it needs to open immediately,” Spanberger said.

She called on President Donald Trump to “demonstrate leadership, bringing people together” and working through negotiations, “whether it’s before or after.”

Sunday marked the 40th day of the shutdown that has left federal workers unpaid, threatened food assistance and disrupted flights across the country.

Senate Democrats have been pushing for an extension of COVID-era tax credits that have made health insurance policies under the Affordable Care Act more affordable for millions of low-income and middle-income Americans. Open enrollment for Obamacare policies began Nov. 1, and many families face a spike in their health insurance premiums with the tax credits set to expire at the end of the year.

Coming ‘energy crisis’

As for rising energy prices, Spanberger said “the increased costs are not driven by the increase in data centers.” She said “bad energy policies” in some neighboring states have driven up energy prices, particularly in Southwest Virginia.

“But looking towards the future,” Spanberger said, “we have to be clear-eyed about the fact that we will have an energy crisis.”

She said that in a rate case pending before the State Corporation Commission, “it will be important that large-scale energy users, particularly data centers,” pay their fair share for the energy they are using.

Spanberger said energy production in Virginia must increase to meet increasing demand, from communities as well as large-scale users.

“It is a real challenge that we have to get ahead of,” Spanberger said.

College boards

She criticized what she termed federal overreach in the Trump administration’s efforts to compel changes — largely related to DEI — at large Virginia universities.

Spanberger has an undergraduate degree from the University of Virginia, where President Jim Ryan resigned under pressure in June.

“On the ground here we did not see our governor in any way step up in defense of our university,” Spanberger said.

“As governor I will be clear-eyed about ensuring that we have structures in place, including boards of visitors across our universities that want to defend academic freedom and, frankly, the viability and vitality of extraordinary institutions like the University of Virginia.”

Brennan, the moderator, also asked Spanberger whether the election of Zohran Mamdani, a self-described “Democratic Socialist,” as New York’s mayor hurts the Democrats’ brand.

Spanberger said she campaigned on addressing rising costs and “stopping the chaos” coming from Washington.

“I’m a capitalist, I’m a Democrat and I won by 15 points in Virginia,” Spanberger said.
