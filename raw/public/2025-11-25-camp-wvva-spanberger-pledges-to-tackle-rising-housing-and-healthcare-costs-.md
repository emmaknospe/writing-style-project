By Heather Olinger

RICHMOND, Va. (WVVA) – Governor-elect Abigail Spanberger told “Morning Joe” that her top priority as the 75th governor of Virginia will be lowering costs for families across the Commonwealth, with a particular focus on housing and healthcare. She described a widespread sense of unease among renters, first-time homebuyers and working families as rents and home prices continue to rise.

Speaking on the program, Spanberger pointed to data she said drives home the problem: rents in Virginia have increased 14 percent over the past four years, while the cost of buying a home has gone up 18 percent.

Everywhere I travel across Virginia, in the reddest of the red areas and the bluest of the blue, people have been clear-eyed about the fact that there is this feeling of uneasiness that even when they can still afford to make that payment at the pharmacy counter or the grocery store, they worry about what comes tomorrow. – VA Governor-elect Abigail Spanberger

Spanberger framed those numbers not as abstract statistics but as daily pressures that shape family decisions, workforce mobility and local economies. She said lowering housing costs will include policies aimed at easing the burden on renters and supporting first-time homebuyers.

Rural healthcare at risk

Spanberger also sounded the alarm about rural healthcare in Virginia, linking recent closures of clinics and hospitals to tax changes she said amplify cost pressures. She criticized what she called the Trump-era tax law and a measure she referred to as the “One Big Beautiful Bill,” saying both have contributed to higher insurance and prescription costs and jeopardized rural care.

The governor-elect noted that three rural clinics in Virginia have already closed and warned that no fewer than six rural hospitals are on a path toward closure unless steps are taken to stabilize funding and reimbursements. She highlighted Lee County Community Hospital as an example of why rural facilities matter: the hospital reopened in 2021 after years of shuttered services and community efforts to preserve the building, but now faces renewed financial vulnerability.

A reopened hospital can be an extraordinary beacon, not just of hope, but of employment, of healthcare, of connected community. – VA Governor-elect Abigail Spanberger

She argued that preserving rural hospitals is vital to the economic health and future prosperity of communities across Virginia.

Why this matters for Virginians

Housing affordability and healthcare access are linked to workforce retention, economic growth and public health. High housing costs make it harder for employers to attract workers. Loss of rural healthcare access increases travel times for routine care and emergency services, raises uncompensated care burdens on nearby hospitals and can accelerate population decline in smaller communities.

Spanberger’s emphasis on both issues signals that the incoming administration plans to prioritize measures that touch everyday costs for families. Specific proposals were not laid out in detail during the interview, but Spanberger framed the problems as bipartisan concerns that require practical solutions at the state level.
