By Michael Martz

Gov.-elect Abigail Spanberger began filling her Cabinet on Tuesday, naming Marvin Figueroa as secretary of health and human resources with the state bracing for major changes to Medicaid and Virginia’s health insurance marketplace under President Donald Trump.

Figueroa, who was one of seven co-chairs on Spanberger’s transition team, is an old hand at health care policy in Richmond. He served for three years as deputy secretary of health and human resources under then-Gov. Ralph Northam.

He helped guide Virginia’s expansion of Medicaid at the beginning of 2019, shape the state’s health insurance marketplace under the Affordable Care Act and carry out the state’s initial response to the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020.

“Virginia’s Department of Health and Human Resources oversees 12 state agencies and supports some of the most vulnerable communities across our commonwealth — including children, senior citizens and Virginians with disabilities,” Spanberger said in announcing the appointment Tuesday.

“As governor, I am committed to making sure our neighbors have access to safe, reliable health care and are served by leaders who are ready to tackle the tough challenges ahead,” she said. “I know that Mr. Figueroa will always put the health and safety of Virginians first — and I look forward to working alongside him to deliver affordable, accessible care for every Virginian.”

Figueroa, who also worked as senior adviser on health care to U.S. Sen. Mark Warner, D-Va., acknowledged the challenges that the new administration faces.

“The challenges are real,” he said Tuesday. “Families are feeling the strain of rising costs, uneven access to care and systems that are too often hard to navigate.”

“I share the governor-elect’s commitment to meet these issues with the urgency Virginians deserve,” he said. “I am ready to work with the General Assembly, our agencies and partners across the Commonwealth to strengthen the systems communities rely on and to deliver progress people can feel in their daily lives.”

A native of Honduras, Figueroa lives in Arlington County. He has a bachelor’s degree from Vanderbilt University and a master’s degree from Harvard University. He is currently vice president of the health and life sciences practice at BGR Group, a national public affairs firm based in Washington, D.C.

State, federal experience

After leaving state government in 2021, he worked for three years as director of intergovernmental and external affairs at the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services under then-President Joe Biden. He then served as chief of staff to U.S. Sen. Laphonza Butler, D-Calif.

Spanberger will be inaugurated Jan. 17 as Virginia’s 75th governor. Figueroa is the first member of her Cabinet she has announced, with more expected later this week.

He brings experience to the incoming administration under Spanberger, a former three-term congresswoman who is new to state government.

Her immediate staff — Chief of Staff Bonnie Krenz-Schnurman and transition director Karen Mask — were longtime aides to her in Congress, but her transition and policy teams include people with experience in Virginia government, such as Figueroa.

Health care funding

Figueroa figures to be in the middle of one of Virginia’s biggest policy and budget debates as state policymakers cope with big changes in federal funding of health care and social services.

The budget reconciliation bill that Trump signed into law July 4 makes significant cuts in federal funding of Medicaid, food assistance and other safety net programs to pay for expanded tax cuts.

It also does not extend enhanced subsidies for health insurance, leaving hundreds of thousands of Virginians at risk of not being able to afford health care on Jan. 1. The issue was not resolved during a 43-day federal government shutdown, which Senate Democrats triggered by rejecting a Republican stopgap spending bill that didn’t address health care costs. The stopgap funding is set to expire Jan. 30, setting up another potential federal budget showdown in Congress.

If Congress does not act on health insurance subsidies, it would cost Virginia about $200 million over the next two years to fill the gap, the House Appropriations Committee estimates.

Virginia already faces a potential $360 million bill over the next three years to make up the diminished federal share of funding for the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, known as SNAP, after the budget bill shifted some of the cost to states.

Medicaid already is becoming a much larger piece of the state budget, with an additional $3.2 billion needed this year and the next two-year budget to maintain existing services.

The $26 billion program faces even bigger challenges in the next two-year budget cycle, when federal budget cuts will force Medicaid recipients to comply with a new work requirement and renew their eligibility for benefits every six months instead of annually. The Trump tax cut bill counts on big reductions in Medicaid enrollment for savings.

The federal legislation will also cut direct-care payments to health care providers, which are a critical component of the 2018 budget deal to expand Virginia’s Medicaid program. As a result, health care clinics have already begun to close in rural areas of the state.

“Virginia will face exponentially higher costs — particularly from changes to the Medicaid and SNAP programs — beginning in the (Fiscal Year) 2028-2030 biennium,” House Appropriations Director Anne Oman said at the committee’s budget retreat last month.
