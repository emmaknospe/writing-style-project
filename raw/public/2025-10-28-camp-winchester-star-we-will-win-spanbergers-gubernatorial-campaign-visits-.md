By Brian Brehm

Abigail Spanberger’s Democratic campaign for Virginia governor rolled into Winchester Tuesday morning for a breakfast rally that packed the Railway Café restaurant at 430 N. Cameron St.

“One week from today, people across Virginia who haven’t already early voted … will head to the polls,” Spanberger told more than 100 supporters at the rally. “They’ll head to the polls with a choice before them of what it is that they want for the future of our commonwealth.”

Spanberger, a former federal law enforcement officer, undercover CIA agent and three-term member of the U.S. House of Representatives for Virginia’s 7th Congressional District, did not arrive alone Tuesday as she stumped in the backyard of her Republican gubernatorial opponent, Winsome Earle-Sears of Frederick County. Joining her were Democratic Stephens City Mayor Mike Diaz, Democratic candidates for the Virginia House of Delegates Jon Lucci (32nd District) and Shane Boswell (31st District), and independent and former U.S. representative for Virginia’s 5th Congressional District Denver Riggleman.

“This is what Democracy looks like, right?” Diaz said as the crowd cheered and waved signs supporting Spanberger’s campaign.

Riggleman’s attendance was somewhat surprising. When he served in Congress from 2019 to 2021, he was a Republican. He left the GOP and became an independent when his two-year term ended, after having stated the Virginia Republican Party was broken. On Tuesday morning, Boswell jokingly referred to Riggleman as “the last sane Republican to represent the 5th.”

“I hope you’re OK with a former Republican addressing you today,” Riggleman said before telling the audience that he and Spanberger, despite being members of opposing political parties, worked together in Congress, particularly on a bill to expand broadband internet to rural communities.

“That was the first bipartisan thing I’d done,” Riggleman said of House Resolution 8368, the Rural Broadband Expansion Act of 2020. “I was a little worried about it … because I was in a very moderate caucus called the Freedom Caucus at that time.”

Attendees laughed when Riggleman called the Freedom Caucus “moderate,” as it’s considered to be the most conservative of all the blocs in the U.S. House of Representatives.

Working with Spanberger to support the broadband bill, Riggleman said, “was the first time I got in a lot of trouble because Republican committees at that time didn’t want to do any business with Democrats.”

“If I can build a coalition with this gentleman right here,” Spanberger joked as she put her arm around Riggleman, “anything is possible. … The reality is, he is someone who I respect deeply and I’m grateful for his friendship because when he found the unacceptable unacceptable, he said so. And at great personal cost to himself and his family.”

Spanberger said on Tuesday morning that her parents taught her the importance of helping others and building communities.

“My mother, a nurse and the consummate volunteer in all things. My father, an Army veteran and a career federal law enforcement officer,” she said. “They used to instill in my sisters and me the idea that if you see something wrong, it is your duty to fix it.”

When Spanberger was in Congress, she said she put people before politics. That approach earned her The Lugar Center’s designation of being one of the most bipartisan representatives in the House during her six years of service.

“What I did was focus on understanding the challenges that our neighbors were facing, that farmers were facing, business owners, teachers, educators, advocates, community members were facing, and I went about trying to address those issues at the federal level,” Spanberger said. “I had bills signed into law by President Trump in his first term and by President Biden, and I’m proud of the work that I did.”

In 2023, Spanberger said she wanted to start focusing her energies on improving her home state of Virginia, and the best place to do that was in Richmond.

“And so I decided to run for governor, launched my campaign back in ’23, finished up my term [in the House of Representatives] the beginning of January of this year and I’ve been running full time ever since,” she said.

If elected, Spanberger said she will use the governor’s office to protect all residents of the commonwealth. That includes the 320,000 federal employees that live in the state, many of whom have gone unpaid since a shutdown of the federal government began on Oct. 1 due to Republicans and Democrats being able to agree on a budget bill.

“Attacks on the federal workforce impact not just federal workers and their families and their communities, but the entirety of Virginia’s economy,” she said.

Spanberger also criticized the effects that the Trump administration’s Big Beautiful Bill will have on the commonwealth, including a projected loss of $2 billion per year for Virginia hospitals due to cuts to federal Medicaid reimbursements that could lead to facility closures, service reductions and higher insurance premiums.

“I outline these challenges because it’s in a moment like this that we need a governor who will stand up for Virginia,” Spanberger said. “Now, the reality is that the governor of Virginia cannot stop every bad policy coming out of this White House or coming out of this Congress. But the governor of Virginia can start by naming and numbering the impact on people, on communities, on our economy, and force others to stand up as well. And the governor of Virginia can work to mitigate some of the worst harms in healthcare.”

Lucci implored the crowd to amp up their efforts on behalf of Spanberger and Virginia’s Democratic ticket heading into Election Day.

“If you have anything left in the tank energy-wise, please run to the finish line,” Lucci said. “Go out and knock on doors, make calls, do everything you can to help us change the world.”

“I am excited about what I know will happen on Tuesday,” Spanberger said, “which is that we will win.”

The winner of the race will be the first woman elected governor of Virginia.

Election Day is Tuesday, Nov. 4. The last day for early in-person voting is Saturday, Nov. 1.
