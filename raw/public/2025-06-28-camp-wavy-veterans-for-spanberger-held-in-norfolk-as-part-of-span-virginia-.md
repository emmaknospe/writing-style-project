By Ethan Krauss

NORFOLK, Va. (WAVY) – Democratic gubernatorial candidate Abigail Spanberger made over 40 stops across Virginia this week. On Saturday, she traveled through Hampton Roads, making stops on the south side and the Peninsula. All of this is part of her “Span Virginia Bus Tour.”

Saturday was day 8 of 8 on the tour, making Hampton Roads the last stop. During the “Veterans for Spanberger” event in Norfolk, the former congresswoman spoke to Virginia veterans, outlining what she would do for them if elected governor.

“As governor, I will work to lower housing costs, making our Commonwealth an affordable place to live. I will put our veterans first by cracking down on the predatory actors and claim sharks that are taking advantage of our Commonwealth’s veterans. I will put Virginia veterans first by providing consistent and reliable access to mental health services, and I will reestablish the Governor’s Coordinating Council on Homelessness,” said Spanberger.

Spanberger was joined by Virginia Delegate Don Scott, former governor Ralph Northam and candidates for attorney general and lieutenant governor Jay Jones and Ghazala Hashmi. All had the same missions in mind, sweeping the November elections and putting veterans first.

“Donald Trump said, ‘I’m going to cut federal funding.’ That would impact our health care, our children, our veterans. We deserve better,” said former delegate and Attorney General candidate Jay Jones. “With this Democratic ticket, you get a united front, people who will put veterans first.”

Veterans who attended say they’re optimistic about Spanberger’s promises.

“I love that there are so many veterans here, and there’s so much support for her,” said Air Force veteran Kathy Dalesandre. “We were already not getting great care, and now the cuts to the VA are just going to cut us even more. For me, the mental health is most important, and she understands, future governor, hopefully Spanberger, understands the struggles that we go through right now. And, and so taking care for us.”

Spanberger and her fellow democratic nominees will now look towards the November elections, hoping to perform a blue sweep for the state of Virginia.
