RICHMOND, VA — Governor Spanberger yesterday highlighted Virginia’s rise to #3 in CNBC’s “America’s Top States for Business” rankings — and pledged to continue her bipartisan focus on attracting new investment, lowering costs for families and businesses, and building a stronger, more resilient economy in the face of economic uncertainty out of Washington.

In its annual rankings, CNBC spotlighted Virginia’s rise, including the Commonwealth’s continued investments in world-class infrastructure, commitment to education and workforce training, and improved cost of living. Since taking office in January, Governor Spanberger has signed a host of bipartisan laws to make life more affordable for Virginians, announced more than $5 billion in new business investment and 3,000 new jobs, and worked to set up students and other Virginians for success — including by investing in apprenticeships and launching InternshipsVA, a new statewide initiative to connect students with paid internships.

Read below for coverage of the Commonwealth’s rise in CNBC’s “America’s Top States for Business” rankings.

Virginia Mercury: Virginia climbs to third spot in CNBC ‘Top States for Business’ rankings

Virginia shifted from fourth to third place in CNBC’s annual “top states for business” rankings, Gov. Abigail Spanberger announced Thursday.

States that achieve the top bracket are considered the best in the nation for attracting or retaining companies and supporting regional economies.

“I think the fact that we are on the rise to number three means that we’re moving in the right direction,” Spanberger said in a phone interview Thursday afternoon.

“It isn’t just that the cost of doing business has improved; it’s that the cost of living has improved,” she said, citing a slate of new laws aimed at addressing housing, healthcare and energy affordability she signed this year.

The CNBC ranking can serve as a progress report card for governors and lawmakers while also helping states shape new business goals.

Washington Business Journal: Virginia climbs to No. 3 in CNBC's state business ranking after federal cuts dragged it down

Virginia is back in the top three in CNBC’s annual ranking of the top states for business, an annual rating that governors and the economic development set have long taken great pride in.

The commonwealth checked in at No. 3 this year, up one spot from the year prior. Virginia held the No. 1 spot in 2024 but slipped to No. 4 in 2025 when the state’s economy was dinged by its exposure to President Donald Trump’s cuts to the federal government.
