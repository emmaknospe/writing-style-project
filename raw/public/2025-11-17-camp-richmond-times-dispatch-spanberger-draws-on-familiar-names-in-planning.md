By Michael Martz

Gov.-elect Abigail Spanberger may be a newcomer to state politics in Richmond, but she’s calling on state capital veterans for advice on her policy agenda before her inauguration as Virginia’s 75th governor on Jan. 17.

Spanberger said Friday that she has named 39 people to teams in 15 policy areas of state government, from administration to veterans and defense affairs, and many of the names are familiar. Many of them are former state government officials under two former Democratic governors, Ralph Northam and Terry McAuliffe, whose wife sits on one of the teams.

“To deliver on the leadership that Virginians have made clear they want — leadership that will always put Virginia first — the commonwealth needs experts at the table, and these Virginians bring valuable expertise from various sectors that help them understand the challenges facing communities across the commonwealth,” Spanberger said.

Aubrey Layne, former secretary of finance and transportation under Northam and McAuliffe, respectively, is back to advise the new governor on crucial issues of state finance, just as he did with Republican Gov. Glenn Youngkin four years ago.

Layne is now executive vice president and chief administrative officer at Sentara Health in Hampton Roads. The team handling health and human resources issues includes former state Medicaid director Karen Kimsey; former first lady Dorothy McAuliffe; and Dr. Cameron Webb, a physician at the University of Virginia and former Democratic candidate for Congress in the 5th District.

Speaking of UVa, Spanberger’s education team includes Connor Andrews, the university’s state government relations director. It also includes former Virginia Secretary of Education Dietra Trent and Anne Hyslop, director of policy development at All4Ed.

Another former Cabinet secretary, Grindly Johnson, will handle administration policy. Johnson was secretary of administration under Northam and deputy secretary of transportation under McAuliffe.

In the critical issue of energy, the governor-elect is tapping former State Corporation Commission Judge Angela Navarro, who also served as deputy secretary of both natural resources and commerce and trade under Northam and McAuliffe, respectively. She’s now president of Align Energy Advisers.

Spanberger also is drawing heavily on the Southern Environmental Law Center in Charlottesville for advice on energy and natural resources. She named Josephus Allmond, a staff attorney at the center, and former SELC attorney Will Cleveland, now principal at Lighthouse Policy and Law, to her energy team.

She also picked Cale Jaffe, a UVa law professor who previously worked at the environmental law center, to her team on natural resources. He is joined by Del. Alfonso Lopez, D-Arlington, chairman of the House Committee on Agriculture, Chesapeake and Natural Resources, and Trieste Lockwood, president of Lockwood Strategies, a clean energy advocate who previously advised Northam and the Department of Environmental Quality.

Housing, another of Spanberger’s major focuses for reducing costs, will feature a team that includes Bill Shelton, former director of the Department of Housing and Community Development; Falls Church Mayor Letty Hardi; and Nichele Carver, former associate director at the state housing and community development agency and principal at NJC Strategic Planning and Consulting.

In transportation, the team includes Nick Donohue, a former deputy secretary of transportation under McAuliffe and Northam. He is a consultant to legislative and regional initiatives to address funding needs at Washington’s Metro system and other public transit companies in Northern Virginia.

He is joined on the team by longtime state lobbyist Marianne Radcliff, president of Kemper Consulting in Virginia Beach, and Tracy Baynard, a senior vice president at McGuireWoods Consulting, an offshoot of Virginia’s largest law firm.

Speaking of McGuireWoods, Chris Lloyd, senior vice president of infrastructure and economic development at the consulting firm, will serve on the economic and small business team. Joining him are Carrie Chenery, principal at Valley Pike Partners; Maryam Janani-Flores, principal at MJF Strategies and former chief of staff at the U.S. Economic Development Administration; and Jim Moye, senior vice president and counsel at the American Bankers Association.

Former Secretary of Agriculture and Forestry Basil Gooden will serve on the team for his commerce and trade sector, along with Katie Frazier, chief brand officer for Farm Credit of the Virginias; and Kyle Shreve, vice president of external affairs for the Virginia, Maryland, Delaware Association of Electric Cooperatives.

Labor also will play a prominent role in the coming Democratic administration. Spanberger’s team includes Greg Ackerman, president of the Baltimore-D.C. Metro Building Trades Council; Shawn Avery, president and CEO of the Hampton Roads Workforce Council; David Broder, executive director of SEIU Virginia State Council, representing service workers; and Paige Shevlin, former adviser for infrastructure workforce development at the U.S. Department of Transportation.

In public safety and homeland security, former state emergency management coordinator Curtis Brown will serve on a team that also includes Jae K Davenport, former deputy secretary of public safety and homeland security and now president of Davenport Strategies LLC; and Mike Harvey, executive director of the Rappahannock Regional Criminal Justice Academy.

Former deputy secretary of the commonwealth Traci DeShazor will serve on the team for those issues in that Cabinet secretariat, along with MacKenzie Heidelmark, former constituent casework manager in Spanberger’s 7th District congressional office.

On policy affecting military veterans and defense affairs, former Secretary of Veterans and Defense Affairs Carlos Hopkins, now a partner at Gentry Locke law firm, will serve with Carl Bedell, a lawyer, former U.S. Army captain and former chair of the Virginia Board of Veterans Services. Bedell also was a Democratic candidate in the 7th Congressional District primary last year.

Finally, Spanberger turned to David Cary, former chief of staff to both first lady Pamela Northam and House Speaker Eileen Filler-Corn, to handle policy for the first gentleman, Adam Spanberger.
