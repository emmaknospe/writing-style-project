By Princess Harrell

Democratic gubernatorial candidate Abigail Spanberger unveiled her “Growing Virginia Plan” on Tuesday, outlining a proposal to grow the state’s economy by investing in apprenticeships, job training, and workforce development, starting with students as early as middle school.

The three-term congresswoman detailed her plan during a campaign stop in Prince William County on Wednesday, standing alongside electricians, labor leaders, and lawmakers who support a more inclusive approach to economic growth.

“We will work with Virginia’s K-12 schools, community colleges, and four-year universities to promote career and technical education programs that provide students with meaningful, career-ready experiences,” Spanberger said. “This is about preparing Virginians for high-demand, good-paying careers.”

The plan includes expanding access to registered apprenticeships, paid internships, dual-enrollment courses, and on-campus career centers, especially in rural and underserved communities.

IBEW Local Union 26 business manager Chris Cash praised the plan’s focus on workers.

“We have an incredible opportunity to bring more young people than ever into the skilled trades,” he said. “And a pathway which lifts them up into the middle class.”

Travis Brown, an electrician and graduate of IBEW Local Union 26’s apprenticeship program, credited the training with changing his life.

“Every apprentice that walks through these doors is given the tools and the support that they need to succeed, not just in the workforce, but also in life,” Brown said.

Spanberger’s plan also addresses recent federal job losses, including at the Department of Defense. Del. Josh Thomas, D-Prince William, said the state could face a $6 billion hit to its GDP due to these cuts.

To assist impacted workers, Spanberger proposes using the Virginia Board of Workforce Development to help match laid-off federal employees with jobs in the private sector based on their existing skills.

In addition to workforce development, the plan calls for:

“We have an incredible opportunity to bring more young people into the skilled trades and into a pathway which lifts them up into the middle class,” said Chris Cash, business manager for IBEW Local Union 26.

Spanberger also pledged to streamline permitting processes, support small businesses, invest in Virginia’s ports, and promote agricultural and tourism exports. She said Virginia’s global competitiveness will be key to its long-term success.

The candidate was also critical of recent federal actions, which she said have destabilized Virginia’s workforce.

“In the months since the Trump administration launched DOGE, I’ve been hearing from Virginians across the political spectrum, Virginians who have lost their jobs, left their jobs early, or are worried their jobs will be targeted next,” Spanberger said.

She said the state must do more to protect those workers, including legal support and job placement services. She emphasized leveraging Virginia’s strengths in cybersecurity, AI, and advanced manufacturing.

“Programs like these set people up for careers in good-paying jobs that grow our economy and our communities,” she said.
